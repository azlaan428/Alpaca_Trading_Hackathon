1.1. Abstract
This document is the authoritative naming-convention specification for VoiceGate, covering
variables, functions/methods, classes, decorators, custom exceptions, and constants, as
explicitly required to be included by the user, extending the PEP 8 baseline already
established in 005_voicegate_coding_standards.txt Section 1.9.

1.2. Key Words
naming convention, snake_case, PascalCase, UPPER_SNAKE_CASE, decorator, custom exception,
PEP 8, identifier clarity.

1.3. Executive Summary
VoiceGate follows standard PEP 8 Python naming conventions throughout, with project-specific
clarifications: exceptions are suffixed `Error`, decorators are named as verbs or verb
phrases describing their effect, constants are declared in `UPPER_SNAKE_CASE` in dedicated
constants modules (never as inline magic values, per 005_voicegate_coding_standards.txt
Section 1.9), and every identifier name must describe what it represents or does without
requiring the reader to consult external context.

1.4. Key Findings
1.4.1. Variables and function/method names use `snake_case`, per PEP 8, and must be
descriptive nouns (variables) or verb phrases (functions/methods), never single-letter names
outside of tight, obviously-scoped loops.
1.4.2. Classes use `PascalCase`; value-object classes representing VoiceGate concepts are
named after the concept itself (e.g. `Persona`, `Scenario`, `Verdict`, `RuleCitation`), not
prefixed/suffixed with implementation detail words like "Manager" or "Data" unless that word
adds real meaning.
1.4.3. Custom exceptions are `PascalCase`, suffixed with `Error` (e.g.
`GuardrailViolationError`, `InvalidPersonaConfigurationError`), and each maps to exactly one
rule-violation or guardrail-violation category, never a generic catch-all exception.
1.4.4. Decorators are named as a verb or verb phrase describing the behavior they add (e.g.
`@validate_command`, `@log_event`), in `snake_case`, consistent with function-naming rules
since a decorator is itself a function.
1.4.5. Constants are `UPPER_SNAKE_CASE` and live in dedicated constants modules rather than
inline in engine logic, per 005_voicegate_coding_standards.txt Section 1.9's "no magic
numbers, no hardcoded strings" rule.

1.5. Philosophical Preamble
A name is the first and cheapest piece of documentation a reader ever encounters. This
document exists because the user explicitly required it to be included, and because
consistent naming is what makes the rest of this corpus's documentation-heavy discipline
(WHAT/WHY/HOW docstrings, per 005_voicegate_coding_standards.txt Section 1.8) actually
readable in practice rather than merely present — which matters especially for a compliance
tool where a reviewer must trust, at a glance, that `VerdictEngine` really does produce
verdicts and nothing else.

1.6. Variable Naming
1.6.1. `snake_case`, descriptive nouns: `transcript`, `caller_persona`, `compliance_score`,
not `t`, `cp`, `cs`.
1.6.2. Boolean variables are named as a predicate: `is_compliant`, `has_flag`,
`requires_human_review`.
1.6.3. Loop counters may use short names (`i`, `idx`, `row`) only within a tightly scoped,
obviously bounded loop; anything crossing a function boundary must use a descriptive name.

1.7. Function and Method Naming
1.7.1. `snake_case`, verb or verb-phrase: `score_call()`, `select_scenario()`,
`draw_persona()`, `render_verdict()`.
1.7.2. Functions returning a boolean are named as a predicate: `is_call_compliant()`,
`has_reached_verdict()`.
1.7.3. Private/internal helper functions not part of a module's public surface are prefixed
with a single leading underscore: `_apply_variance()`.

1.8. Class Naming
1.8.1. `PascalCase`, named after the concept represented: `Persona`, `Scenario`, `Verdict`,
`Transcript`, `CallOrchestrator`, `ComplianceScoringEngine`, consistent with the module names
in 006_voicegate_scaffolding.txt Section 1.6.
1.8.2. Service/engine classes are suffixed with their role where that adds clarity (`Engine`,
`Service`, `Adviser`): `VerdictEngine`, `RngService`, `PatchAdviser`.

1.9. Decorator Naming
1.9.1. `snake_case`, verb phrase describing the added behavior: `@validate_command`,
`@log_event`, `@require_active_run`.
1.9.2. A decorator must not be named after its implementation mechanism (e.g. `@wrapper`,
`@decorator1`); it must be named after the behavior it adds to the decorated function.

1.10. Custom Exception Naming
1.10.1. `PascalCase`, suffixed `Error`: `GuardrailViolationError`,
`InvalidPersonaConfigurationError`, `InvalidScenarioTargetError`,
`RuleCitationNotFoundError`, `PostVerdictActionError`.
1.10.2. Each custom exception maps to exactly one rule-violation or guardrail-violation
category (e.g. `GuardrailViolationError` maps to a detected prompt-injection or
actor/speaker-role-confusion attempt in the transcription/scoring engines); a new
rule-violation category requires a new, specifically named exception rather than reuse of a
generic one.
1.10.3. Exceptions never use bare `Exception` or `ValueError`/`TypeError` directly in
compliance-rule-violation or guardrail-violation contexts; only in genuinely generic,
non-domain-specific error contexts (e.g. malformed configuration file), per
005_voicegate_coding_standards.txt Section 1.9.

1.11. Constant Naming
1.11.1. `UPPER_SNAKE_CASE`: `DEFAULT_INTENSITY_TIER`, `MINIMUM_TRANSCRIPT_LENGTH`,
`COMPLIANCE_SCORE_FAIL_THRESHOLD`, `STANDARD_CATALOG_CATEGORY_COUNT`.
1.11.2. Constants live in dedicated modules (e.g. `src/data/constants.py` or co-located with
the module they configure), never inline as magic numbers or hardcoded strings, per
005_voicegate_coding_standards.txt Section 1.9.

1.12. Test Naming (Cross-Reference)
Test module and function naming conventions are not restated here to avoid duplication; this
document is the source of truth for production-code naming, a future testing-standards
document for test-code naming.

|--------------------|--------------|-----------|

| Class | PascalCase | `VerdictEngine` |
| Decorator | snake_case verb phrase | `@validate_command` |
