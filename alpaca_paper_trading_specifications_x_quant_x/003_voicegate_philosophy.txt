1.1. Abstract
This document states the design philosophy underlying VoiceGate: why an automated red-team/
compliance test bench for voice AI agents must be guardrail-first, advisory-only in its AI
features, and disciplined about which external claims are confirmed versus
verification-flagged. It extends the philosophical stance first articulated in
voice_transcription_concept/voice_chat_concept.md into concrete, checkable design pillars,
replacing the sibling project's game-design pillars (003_game_philosophy.txt, preserved in
git history).

1.2. Key Words
design philosophy, guardrail-first, prompt injection, model poisoning, deterministic verdict
engine, advisory-only, Suggested Patch Adviser, legibility, verification-flagged citation,
vertical slice, reviewer experience.

1.3. Executive Summary
VoiceGate is built on the premise that a compliance test bench is only as trustworthy as the
guardrails protecting its own judge models: transcript content from either speaker must
always be treated as untrusted DATA, never as instructions to the judge, exactly as retrieved
tool content must never override system instructions. This document names five design
pillars that every future scoring, UI, or engineering decision must be checked against, and
formalizes a verification-flagged citation discipline so that external legal/regulatory
claims (statutes, tribunal precedents) are never asserted as confirmed without independent
verification.

1.4. Key Findings
1.4.1. VoiceGate's core identity is synthesis, not imitation: it deliberately merges
adversarial persona simulation, live call orchestration, dual-channel transcription, and
dual-referent (statute + company Terms & Conditions) compliance scoring.
1.4.2. Randomized variance in VoiceGate (tone jitter, interruption timing, escalation; see
026_randomization_variance_terminology.txt) is always bounded and attributable to a specific,
seeded mechanism — never to opaque AI improvisation of new scoring rules mid-run.
1.4.3. The Suggested Patch Adviser (see 016_voicegate_operating_modes.txt) is philosophically
required to be advisory-only: it may propose a system-prompt patch and explain its rationale,
but it must never apply that patch to the agent under test without explicit human
confirmation, preserving the deterministic-verdict-engine principle.
1.4.4. AI judge models are explicitly known to be capable of actor/speaker-role confusion
(misattributing which line was spoken by the agent under test versus the simulated caller);
guarding against this confusion is a first-class guardrail requirement, not an incidental
detail.
1.4.5. Delivery is explicitly prioritized as a vertical slice (one full gauntlet run,
Test Bench -> Call Review -> Risk Report) over breadth of half-finished scenario coverage,
per 001_voice_transcription_concept.txt Section 1.3.

1.5. Philosophical Preamble
Documentation is a contract between agent and system: precision of structure mirrors
precision of thought. VoiceGate extends that claim from documentation into the product
itself. A tool whose purpose is judging another AI's compliance forfeits its own credibility
the moment it accepts untrusted transcript content as instructions, or asserts an unverified
statute citation as settled fact. Guardrail discipline and citation discipline are therefore
not bureaucratic overhead layered onto the product; they are the product's core value
proposition, in the same way a courtroom's evidentiary rules are not separate from justice
but constitutive of it.

1.6. Design Pillars

1.6.1. Guardrails Protect Trust
Transcript content from either the agent under test or the simulated caller is always treated
as untrusted DATA, never as instructions to the judge model or the Suggested Patch Adviser.
Prompt-injection and model-poisoning resistance are validated before a run is marked
authoritative (see 028_statutory_ruleset_integration.txt for the rule-citation boundary this
protects).

1.6.2. Controlled Adversarial Variance
Every randomized variance element (tone jitter, interruption timing, escalation intensity) is
seeded and replayable for testing and regression re-runs (see
026_randomization_variance_terminology.txt), so adversarial unpredictability is always
reproducible, not unaccountable.

1.6.3. Deterministic Verdict Engine, Advisory Patch Adviser
The verdict engine (017_voicegate_scoring_rules.txt) is the sole authority over Pass/Fail/
Flag outcomes; the Suggested Patch Adviser recommends a remediation, it never decides or
applies one. Every proposed patch requires explicit human confirmation and a regression
re-run before it can be considered verified.

1.6.4. Legibility and Citation as a First-Class Requirement
Every verdict must be explainable in one plain-language sentence with an explicit rule or
clause citation (see 002_voicegate_aesthetics.txt Section 1.6.4 for the display requirement,
and Section 1.8 below for the citation discipline itself).

1.6.5. Vertical Slice Before Feature Breadth
One complete gauntlet run through all five call-lifecycle phases (Greeting, Intent Discovery,
Resolution Attempt, Compliance Check, Wrap-up; see 017_voicegate_scoring_rules.txt Section
1.6) with a real verdict outperforms broad but shallow scenario coverage at demo time.

1.7. Reviewer-Experience Principles
1.7.1. A compliance reviewer should be able to understand why a call failed within one
Call Review screen view, without needing to read the underlying statute text directly.
1.7.2. Every action that would apply an AI-suggested change (e.g. accepting a Suggested
Patch Adviser recommendation) requires a two-step confirm (review, then confirm), never a
single accidental click (see 004_voicegate_ui_navigation.txt).
1.7.3. Every verdict is explainable in one plain-language sentence plus its rule citation in
the Risk Report (see 020_voicegate_logging_standards.txt for the underlying log-record
standard).

1.8. Verification-Flagged Citation Discipline
External legal/regulatory claims referenced anywhere in this corpus — including EU AI Act
Article 50, California SB 243, and the Air Canada tribunal precedent (chatbot-invented
refund policy held enforceable) — are treated as VERIFICATION-FLAGGED: cited for design
context, not asserted as independently confirmed legal analysis. Any document invoking one of
these citations must retain the VERIFICATION-FLAGGED label rather than upgrading it to
confirmed without an explicit legal-review sign-off, consistent with
0000_documentation_standards.txt Section 1.6.6's hallucination-envelope discipline applied
here to external-citation risk rather than invented game mechanics.

1.11. Risk and Mitigation Table
| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|------|------------|--------|------------|
| A future feature request violates the advisory-only pillar (e.g. "let the Adviser auto-patch") | Medium | High | 1.6.3 and 016_voicegate_operating_modes.txt jointly forbid unattended patch application |
| Judge model actor/role confusion producing a mis-scored verdict | Medium | High | 1.4.4 and 017_voicegate_scoring_rules.txt guardrail sections jointly require role-confusion checks before a verdict is authoritative |

| Feature breadth creep undermining the vertical-slice principle | High | High | 1.6.5 plus 001_voice_transcription_concept.txt Section 1.9 (MVP scope) jointly gate new feature requests |

1.13. Conclusion
VoiceGate's philosophy is that guardrail discipline and citation discipline are the product,
not overhead on top of it. Every future scoring, UI, or engineering decision should be
checkable against the five pillars in Section 1.6 before it is accepted.
