\begin{abstract}
This monograph develops the complete formal proof framework for two
companion documents: (i) \emph{Finite Investment Architecture as
Economic, Financial, Fiscal, and Investment Kalman Filter}, which
establishes the structural isomorphism between the Kalman
prediction--correction cycle and multi-domain capital allocation; and
(ii) \emph{State-of-Charge Analogues for Investment Systems}, which
introduces seven investment state dimensions as hidden variables in
the Kalman model and gates capital deployment through a
product-of-gating-functions architecture.

The monograph proceeds from first principles.  Every definition,
axiom, assumption, and theorem is stated with complete rigour; all
boundary, degenerate, and extremal cases are addressed explicitly;
and all proofs are supplied with sufficient detail so that every
step can be independently verified.  The central results are:

\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*), leftmargin=2.5em,
                  itemsep=2pt, parsep=0pt, topsep=3pt]
  \item The Kalman filter prediction--correction cycle is structurally
        isomorphic to the prior-forecast / evidence-update cycle in
        capital allocation; the isomorphism is established by an
        explicit bijection between the two sets of mathematical objects.
  \item The seven investment states
        $(\calE_t, \calF_t, \calG_t, \calP_t, \calU_t, \calM_t,
        \calT_t)^\top$ form a well-defined hidden state vector in the
        Kalman observation model; agent outputs are the noisy
        observations; and agent confidence determines the inverse
        measurement noise.
  \item The Kalman gain is the investment-domain analogue of the
        confidence-weighted capital deployment fraction; agent
        disagreement inflates the innovation covariance, reducing the
        Kalman gain and therefore capital deployment.
  \item The seven-dimensional state-gating function
        $\prod_{d=1}^{7} g_d(\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)})$ is well-defined,
        non-negative, and equals zero whenever any state falls below
        its critical threshold, providing a formal circuit-breaker
        architecture.
  \item The composite capital deployment policy
        $\pi^*(\mathbf{X}_t, S_t) = K_t^{(\mathrm{Kalman})} \cdot
        C_{\mathrm{available}} \cdot \prod_{d=1}^{7} g_d(\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)})$
        is the minimum-variance unbiased estimator (MVUE) of the
        latent market state given partial, noisy, multi-domain
        observations, under the stated assumptions.
\end{enumerate}

All results are consistent with and extend the companion documents
\texttt{high\_level\_architecture\_proof.tex} and
\texttt{high\_level\_supplementary\_diversification\_proof.tex}.
\end{abstract}

\section{Foundational Assumptions}

\noindent
The following assumptions are stated in full generality.  Each
assumption is accompanied by its precise mathematical
formulation, its domain of validity, all boundary and edge-case
conditions, and an explicit statement of what fails if the
assumption is relaxed.  Every subsequent result in this
monograph is auditable against this list.

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection*{Assumption A1 — Probability Space}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{assumption}[Probability Space]
\label{asm:prob-space}
A complete filtered probability space
\begin{equation}
  \bigl(\Omega,\;\calF,\;\{\calF_t\}_{t \geq 0},\;\Prob\bigr)
  \label{eq:prob-space}
\end{equation}
is fixed throughout this monograph.  The following conditions
hold jointly and are each individually necessary.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A1.\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*, widest=A1.4.]
  \item \textbf{Sample space.}
        $\Omega$ is a non-empty set; every elementary outcome
        $\omega \in \Omega$ represents a complete realisation of
        all random quantities defined herein.

  \item \textbf{$\sigma$-algebra and completeness.}
        $\calF$ is a $\sigma$-algebra on $\Omega$ such that
        $\Prob$ is complete: if $A \in \calF$ with
        $\Prob(A) = 0$ and $B \subseteq A$, then
        $B \in \calF$ (hence $\Prob(B) = 0$).
        Completeness is required to ensure that
        modifications of measurable processes remain
        measurable.

  \item \textbf{Probability measure.}
        $\Prob : \calF \to [0,1]$ is a $\sigma$-additive
        probability measure satisfying $\Prob(\Omega) = 1$ and
        $\Prob(\emptyset) = 0$.  Boundary cases:
        \begin{align}
          \Prob(A) &= 0 \quad \text{is permitted for any
            $A \in \calF$}; \label{eq:prob-zero} \\
          \Prob(A) &= 1 \quad \text{is permitted for any
            $A \in \calF$}. \label{eq:prob-one}
        \end{align}

  \item \textbf{Filtration and usual conditions.}
        The filtration $\{\calF_t\}_{t \geq 0}$ satisfies the
        \emph{usual conditions}:
        \begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
          \item \emph{Right-continuity:}
                $\calF_t = \bigcap_{s > t} \calF_s$ for all
                $t \geq 0$.
          \item \emph{Completeness:}
                $\calF_0$ (and hence every $\calF_t$) contains
                all $\Prob$-null sets of $\calF$.
          \item \emph{Monotonicity:}
                $s \leq t \Rightarrow \calF_s \subseteq
                \calF_t \subseteq \calF$.
        \end{enumerate}
        Right-continuity is required for the optional sampling
        theorem and for c\`{a}dl\`{a}g path regularity.

  \item \textbf{All random objects defined on this space.}
        Every random variable, stochastic process, and
        probability kernel introduced in subsequent chapters is
        assumed to be $(\calF / \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}))$-measurable
        or, where vector-valued, $(\calF / \mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^n))$-measurable.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Violation consequence.}  If completeness is
dropped, null-set modifications of adapted processes may fail
to be measurable, invalidating the Kalman update equations.
If right-continuity fails, the martingale representation
theorem does not apply, breaking the innovation analysis in
Chapter~\ref{ch:kalman}.
\end{assumption}

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection*{Assumption A2 — Discrete Time Horizon}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{assumption}[Discrete Time Horizon]
\label{asm:time}
The time index set is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{T} \;:=\; \{0,\,1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,T\},
  \qquad T \in \mathbb{N},\quad T \geq 1.
  \label{eq:time-set}
\end{equation}
The following sub-conditions hold.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A2.\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*, widest=A2.5.]
  \item \textbf{Initial time.}
        $t = 0$ is the \emph{initialisation step}.  All prior
        distributions are specified at $t = 0$; no observations
        are processed before this step.  The boundary case
        $t = 0$ is explicitly handled in every recursive update.

  \item \textbf{Terminal time.}
        $T < \infty$ is the finite investment horizon.  The
        assumption $T \geq 1$ ensures at least one update step
        exists.  The case $T = 1$ (single-period model) is a
        valid degenerate case; all results reduce correctly.

  \item \textbf{Adaptedness.}
        All processes $\{X_t\}_{t \in \mathcal{T}}$ are
        $\{\calF_t\}$-adapted, i.e.\
        $X_t$ is $\calF_t$-measurable for every
        $t \in \mathcal{T}$.

  \item \textbf{Uniform step size.}
        Without loss of generality the step size is unity.
        Rescaling to an arbitrary step $\Delta t > 0$
        multiplies the process noise covariance by $\Delta t$
        and leaves all structural results unchanged.

  \item \textbf{Boundary conditions on the index.}
        \begin{align}
          t < 0 &\;\Rightarrow\; \text{undefined (no process
            exists prior to initialisation);} \label{eq:t-lb}\\
          t > T &\;\Rightarrow\; \text{undefined (horizon
            enforced strictly).} \label{eq:t-ub}
        \end{align}
        Any expression referencing $t \notin \mathcal{T}$ is
        treated as vacuously false and does not produce a
        result.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Violation consequence.}  If $T = \infty$, the
finite-horizon optimality results of
Chapter~\ref{ch:composite-objective} require replacement by
their infinite-horizon discounted counterparts; the proofs in
their current form do not extend without modification.
\end{assumption}

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection*{Assumption A3 — Agent Index Set}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{assumption}[Agent Index Set]
\label{asm:agents}
There are $N \in \mathbb{N}$, $N \geq 2$, specialised agents
indexed by the set
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{I} \;:=\; \{1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,N\}.
  \label{eq:agent-index}
\end{equation}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A3.\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*, widest=A3.5.]
  \item \textbf{Minimum cardinality.}
        $N \geq 2$ is required so that the IMM mixing matrix
        (Definition~\ref{def:imm}) is non-trivial and the
        ensemble disagreement covariance
        (Proposition~\ref{prop:disagreement}) is well-defined.
        The degenerate case $N = 1$ reduces the system to a
        single Kalman filter; all IMM results hold vacuously.

  \item \textbf{Agent observation map.}
        Each agent $i \in \mathcal{I}$ is associated with a
        non-empty \emph{monitoring set}
        $\mathcal{D}_i \subseteq \{1,\ldots,d\}$ of state
        dimensions it observes.  Agent $i$ emits the scalar
        observation
        \begin{equation}
          z_{i,t}^{(k)} \;\in\; \mathbb{R},
          \qquad k \in \mathcal{D}_i,\quad t \in \mathcal{T}.
          \label{eq:agent-obs}
        \end{equation}

  \item \textbf{Coverage condition.}
        The union of all monitoring sets covers all state
        dimensions:
        \begin{equation}
          \bigcup_{i=1}^{N} \mathcal{D}_i
          \;=\; \{1,\ldots,d\}.
          \label{eq:coverage}
        \end{equation}
        This ensures no hidden-state dimension is permanently
        unobserved, which is required for asymptotic filter
        stability (Theorem~\ref{thm:filter-stability}).

  \item \textbf{Observability of individual agents.}
        Individual monitoring sets may overlap
        ($\mathcal{D}_i \cap \mathcal{D}_j \neq \emptyset$ is
        permitted) or be disjoint; no uniqueness of coverage is
        required.  Redundant coverage increases robustness.

  \item \textbf{Boundary case — single-dimension agent.}
        The case $|\mathcal{D}_i| = 1$ (agent observes exactly
        one dimension) is the minimal valid case.  The
        observation equation for such an agent degenerates to a
        scalar measurement model, which is handled explicitly
        in Section~\ref{sec:observation-model}.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Violation consequence.}  If the coverage
condition~\eqref{eq:coverage} fails, at least one state
dimension is unobserved; the posterior covariance on that
dimension does not contract, rendering capital allocation
for that dimension unreliable.
\end{assumption}

\subsection*{Assumption A4 — State Dimension}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{assumption}[State Dimension]
\label{asm:dimension}
The hidden state vector has dimension
\begin{equation}
  d \;=\; 7,
  \label{eq:state-dim}
\end{equation}
corresponding to the seven investment state dimensions defined
in Chapter~\ref{ch:seven-states}.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{A4.\arabic*.}, leftmargin=*, widest=A4.4.]
  \item \textbf{Fixed dimension.}
        Unless explicitly stated otherwise, $d = 7$ throughout
        this monograph.  All matrix dimensions are indexed
        accordingly: state matrices are
        $7 \times 7$, observation matrices are
        $m_i \times 7$ where $m_i = |\mathcal{D}_i|$.

  \item \textbf{Component range.}
        Each component of the state vector lies in $[0,1]$:
        \begin{equation}
          X_t \;\in\; [0,1]^7
          \quad \forall\, t \in \mathcal{T}.
          \label{eq:state-range}
        \end{equation}
        The boundary values $0$ and $1$ are attained and
        constitute valid, non-degenerate states corresponding
        to fully depleted and fully saturated investment
        conditions, respectively.

  \item \textbf{Boundary condition at $X_t = 0$.}
        When any component $X_t^{(k)} = 0$, the corresponding
        state is \emph{inactive}.  The gate function
        (Definition~\ref{def:gate}) evaluates to zero for that
        component, enforcing zero capital allocation.  This is
        an intended structural feature, not a degenerate edge
        case.

  \item \textbf{Boundary condition at $X_t = \mathbf{1}$.}
        When any component $X_t^{(k)} = 1$, the corresponding
        state is \emph{saturated}.  Nonlinear saturation
        corrections (Remark~\ref{rem:saturation}) may apply;
        the linear Kalman update remains valid as a
        first-order approximation within a neighbourhood of
        radius $\varepsilon > 0$ around the boundary.
\end{enumerate}

\noindent\textbf{Violation consequence.}  If a state component
escapes $[0,1]$, the probabilistic interpretation as a
normalised charge fails.  A projection step must be applied
after each update step; this is formalised in
Remark~\ref{rem:projection}.
\end{assumption}

\section{The Hidden State Vector}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{definition}[Seven-Dimensional Investment State]
\label{def:hidden-state}
The \emph{hidden investment state vector} at time $t$ is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{X}_t \;=\; \begin{pmatrix}
    \calE_t \\ \calF_t \\ \calG_t \\ \calP_t \\
    \calU_t \\ \calM_t \\ \calT_t
  \end{pmatrix} \;\in\; [0,1]^7,
  \label{eq:hidden-state}
\end{equation}
where each component is the normalised state charge of the
corresponding investment dimension:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{} l l X @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Symbol} & \textbf{Name} & \textbf{Investment Dimension} \\
\midrule
$\calE_t$ & Economic state & GDP engine charge; growth, inflation, credit \\
$\calF_t$ & Financial state & Market liquidity charge; stress, spreads, vol \\
$\calG_t$ & Fiscal state & Stimulus reservoir; government budget \\
$\calP_t$ & Portfolio state & Capital resilience; drawdown, cash, risk \\
$\calU_t$ & Fundamental state & Valuation health; earnings, FCF, leverage \\
$\calM_t$ & Market state & Microstructure quality; bid-ask, depth, flow \\
$\calT_t$ & Sector/Tech state & Structural momentum; innovation, adoption \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Each component $\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)} \in [0,1]$ represents the
current \emph{charge level} of dimension $d$: a value near $1$
indicates a fully charged (favourable) state; a value near $0$
indicates a critically discharged (adverse) state.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Measurability of $\mathbf{X}_t$]
\label{rem:state-measurable}
Each component $\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)}$ is $\calF_t$-measurable
by construction: it is estimated by the specialised state agent
for dimension $d$ from market data available up to and including
time $t$.  The state vector $\mathbf{X}_t$ is therefore
$\calF_t$-measurable and takes values in the compact set
$[0,1]^7 \subset \mathbb{R}^7$.
\end{remark}

\subsection*{Assumptions Underpinning the Observation Model}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{assumption}[Agent Index Set]
\label{asm:agent-index}
There exist $N \geq 1$ agents indexed by
$i \in \mathcal{I} \coloneqq \{1, 2, \ldots, N\}$.
Each agent $i$ is a measurable function of the filtration
$(\mathcal{F}_t)_{t \geq 0}$ and produces exactly one scalar
observation per time step $t$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Observation Row Vector]
\label{asm:obs-vector}
For each agent $i \in \mathcal{I}$, the observation row vector
$\mathbf{h}_i \in \mathbb{R}^7$ is deterministic and
$\mathcal{F}_0$-measurable.  It satisfies $\mathbf{h}_i \neq
\mathbf{0}$, so that each agent observes at least one component
of the hidden state $\mathbf{X}_t$.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Noise Independence]
\label{asm:noise-indep}
The scalar measurement noise terms $\nu_1, \ldots, \nu_N$ are
mutually independent, independent of the state process
$(\mathbf{X}_t)_{t \geq 0}$, and each $\nu_i$ is independent of
$\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$.  Formally:
\begin{align}
  \nu_i &\perp \nu_j \quad \forall\, i \neq j, \label{eq:asm-noise-indep-1}\\
  \nu_i &\perp \mathbf{X}_t \quad \forall\, i, t, \label{eq:asm-noise-indep-2}\\
  \nu_i &\perp \mathcal{F}_{t-1} \quad \forall\, i, t. \label{eq:asm-noise-indep-3}
\end{align}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Confidence and Doubt Parameters]
\label{asm:conf-doubt}
For each agent $i \in \mathcal{I}$, the confidence parameter
$c_i$ and the doubt parameter $d_i$ satisfy:
\begin{align}
  c_i &\in (0,\,1], \label{eq:conf-range}\\
  d_i &\in [0,\,1]. \label{eq:doubt-range}
\end{align}
Both parameters are $\mathcal{F}_t$-adapted and updated at each
time step $t$ based on the agent's recent predictive accuracy.
The baseline noise parameter satisfies $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2
> 0$ and is a fixed, pre-specified system constant.
\end{assumption}

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection*{Formal Definitions}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{definition}[Agent Observation]
\label{def:observation}
Let Assumptions~\ref{asm:agent-index}--\ref{asm:noise-indep}
hold.  Agent $i \in \mathcal{I}$ produces a scalar observation
$z_i \in \mathbb{R}$ of the hidden state $\mathbf{X}_t$ via the
linear model
\begin{equation}
  z_i \;=\; \mathbf{h}_i^\top \mathbf{X}_t \;+\; \nu_i,
  \qquad \nu_i \sim \mathcal{N}(0,\, r_i),
  \label{eq:observation-model}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\mathbf{h}_i \in \mathbb{R}^7$ is the agent's
        deterministic observation row vector (see
        Assumption~\ref{asm:obs-vector});
  \item $r_i > 0$ is the agent's measurement noise variance
        (see Definition~\ref{def:measurement-noise}); and
  \item $\nu_i$ is independent of $\mathbf{X}_t$ and of all
        other noise terms (see Assumption~\ref{asm:noise-indep}).
\end{enumerate}
Consequently, conditional on $\mathbf{X}_t$, the observation
$z_i$ is Gaussian:
\begin{equation}
  z_i \mid \mathbf{X}_t
  \;\sim\;
  \mathcal{N}\!\left(\mathbf{h}_i^\top \mathbf{X}_t,\; r_i\right).
  \label{eq:obs-conditional}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Measurement Noise Variance]
\label{def:measurement-noise}
Let Assumption~\ref{asm:conf-doubt} hold.
The measurement noise variance of agent $i \in \mathcal{I}$ at
time $t$ is defined by
\begin{equation}
  r_i \;\coloneqq\; \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \cdot
  \frac{(1 - c_i)\,(1 + d_i)}{c_i},
  \label{eq:measurement-noise}
\end{equation}
where $c_i \in (0,1]$ is the agent's confidence level,
$d_i \in [0,1]$ is the agent's doubt level, and
$\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 > 0$ is the system baseline noise
parameter.  The formula encodes the following principles:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The numerator factor $(1 - c_i)$ is a \emph{confidence
        penalty}: as $c_i \uparrow 1$ the penalty vanishes.
  \item The numerator factor $(1 + d_i)$ is a \emph{doubt
        amplifier}: as $d_i \uparrow 1$ the noise is doubled
        relative to the doubt-free case.
  \item The denominator $c_i$ provides additional inverse
        scaling: lower confidence inflates noise super-linearly.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection*{Proposition and Full Proof}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{proposition}[Monotonicity and Limiting Behaviour of
Measurement Noise]
\label{prop:noise-monotone}
Let Assumption~\ref{asm:conf-doubt} hold and let $r_i$ be given
by Definition~\ref{def:measurement-noise}.  Then the following
five properties hold for every $i \in \mathcal{I}$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{(Positivity)}
        $r_i > 0$ for all $c_i \in (0,1)$ and $d_i \in [0,1]$,
        and $r_i = 0$ if and only if $c_i = 1$ and $d_i = 0$.
  \item \textbf{(Strict monotone decrease in $c_i$)}
        For fixed $d_i \in [0,1]$, the map
        $c_i \mapsto r_i$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,1]$:
        \[
          c_i < c_j
          \;\Longrightarrow\;
          r_i > r_j.
        \]
  \item \textbf{(Strict monotone increase in $d_i$)}
        For fixed $c_i \in (0,1)$, the map
        $d_i \mapsto r_i$ is strictly increasing on $[0,1]$:
        \[
          d_i < d_j
          \;\Longrightarrow\;
          r_i < r_j.
        \]
  \item \textbf{(Near-perfect observation limit)}
        As $c_i \to 1^{-}$ and $d_i \to 0^{+}$ simultaneously,
        $r_i \to 0$.
  \item \textbf{(Pure-noise limit)}
        As $c_i \to 0^{+}$ (for any fixed $d_i \in [0,1]$),
        $r_i \to +\infty$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We prove each part in turn, working directly from
equation~\eqref{eq:measurement-noise}.  Throughout, define the
auxiliary function
\begin{equation}
  g(c, d) \;\coloneqq\; \frac{(1-c)(1+d)}{c},
  \qquad c \in (0,1],\; d \in [0,1],
  \label{eq:aux-g}
\end{equation}
so that $r_i = \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \cdot g(c_i, d_i)$.
Since $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 > 0$ is a strictly positive
constant, it suffices to analyse the sign, monotonicity, and
limiting behaviour of $g$.

\begin{enumerate}

  % -----------------------------------------------------------
  \item \textbf{Positivity.}

  We verify the sign of each factor in $g(c,d)$ separately.

  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 > 0$ by
          Assumption~\ref{asm:conf-doubt}.
    \item[(b)] $(1 + d) \geq 1 > 0$ for all $d \in [0,1]$,
          since $d \geq 0$.
    \item[(c)] $c > 0$ by Assumption~\ref{asm:conf-doubt}
          (strict lower bound), so $1/c > 0$ and the fraction
          is well-defined and finite.
    \item[(d)] $(1 - c) \geq 0$ for $c \in (0,1]$; moreover
          $(1-c) = 0$ if and only if $c = 1$.
  \end{enumerate}

  Combining (a)--(d):
  \begin{align}
    g(c,d) &= \frac{(1-c)(1+d)}{c} \;\geq\; 0
    \quad \text{for all } c \in (0,1],\; d \in [0,1].
    \label{eq:g-nonneg}
  \end{align}
  Equality $g(c,d) = 0$ requires $(1-c) = 0$, i.e.\ $c = 1$.
  When $c = 1$: $g(1, d) = 0 \cdot (1+d) / 1 = 0$ for all
  $d$; in particular $g(1, 0) = 0$.
  For $c < 1$: $(1-c) > 0$, $(1+d) \geq 1 > 0$, $c > 0$, so
  $g(c,d) > 0$ and hence $r_i > 0$.
  This establishes positivity and the characterisation of the
  zero set.

  % -----------------------------------------------------------
  \item \textbf{Strict monotone decrease in $c_i$.}

  Fix $d \in [0,1]$ and consider $g$ as a function of $c$ alone:
  \begin{equation}
    g(c) \;\coloneqq\; \frac{(1-c)(1+d)}{c}
    \;=\; (1+d)\!\left(\frac{1}{c} - 1\right).
    \label{eq:g-c-only}
  \end{equation}
  This representation is obtained by expanding:
  \begin{align}
    \frac{(1-c)(1+d)}{c}
    &= (1+d)\,\frac{1-c}{c}
     = (1+d)\!\left(\frac{1}{c} - \frac{c}{c}\right)
     = (1+d)\!\left(\frac{1}{c} - 1\right).
    \label{eq:g-expand}
  \end{align}
  Since $(1+d) > 0$ (by (b) above), the sign of the derivative
  is determined by $\tfrac{d}{dc}\!\left(\tfrac{1}{c} - 1\right)$:
  \begin{align}
    \frac{d}{dc}\!\left(\frac{1}{c} - 1\right)
    &= -\frac{1}{c^2} \;<\; 0
    \quad \text{for all } c \in (0,1].
    \label{eq:dc-1-over-c}
  \end{align}
  Therefore:
  \begin{align}
    \frac{\partial g}{\partial c}
    &= (1+d) \cdot \left(-\frac{1}{c^2}\right)
     = -\frac{1+d}{c^2} \;<\; 0,
    \label{eq:dg-dc}
  \end{align}
  where the strict inequality holds because $(1+d) \geq 1 > 0$
  and $c^2 > 0$.  A strictly negative derivative on the open
  interval $(0,1]$ implies that $g$ (and hence $r_i$) is
  strictly decreasing in $c_i$.

  % -----------------------------------------------------------
  \item \textbf{Strict monotone increase in $d_i$.}

  Fix $c \in (0,1)$ and consider $g$ as a function of $d$ alone:
  \begin{equation}
    g(d) \;\coloneqq\; \frac{(1-c)(1+d)}{c}.
    \label{eq:g-d-only}
  \end{equation}
  Differentiate with respect to $d$:
  \begin{align}
    \frac{\partial g}{\partial d}
    &= \frac{(1-c)}{c} \cdot
       \frac{d}{dd}(1+d)
     = \frac{(1-c)}{c} \cdot 1
     = \frac{1-c}{c}.
    \label{eq:dg-dd}
  \end{align}
  We verify the sign:
  \begin{align}
    c &\in (0,1)
    \;\Longrightarrow\; 1 - c \in (0,1)
    \;\Longrightarrow\; 1 - c > 0,
    \label{eq:one-minus-c-pos}\\
    c &> 0
    \;\Longrightarrow\; \frac{1-c}{c} > 0.
    \label{eq:frac-pos}
  \end{align}
  Hence $\tfrac{\partial g}{\partial d} > 0$ strictly, so $g$
  (and hence $r_i$) is strictly increasing in $d_i$ whenever
  $c_i \in (0,1)$.

  \textbf{Boundary case $c_i = 1$.}  When $c_i = 1$,
  $g(1,d) = 0$ for all $d$; the map $d \mapsto g(1,d)$ is
  identically zero and hence not strictly increasing.  However,
  by Assumption~\ref{asm:conf-doubt}, $c_i \in (0,1]$; the
  statement of Part~3 explicitly restricts to $c_i \in (0,1)$,
  so this degenerate case is excluded.

  % -----------------------------------------------------------
  \item \textbf{Near-perfect observation limit.}

  We evaluate the limit of $g(c,d)$ as $c \to 1^{-}$ and
  $d \to 0^{+}$ independently (since the limit factorises):
  \begin{align}
    \lim_{\substack{c \to 1^{-} \\ d \to 0^{+}}} g(c,d)
    &= \lim_{\substack{c \to 1^{-} \\ d \to 0^{+}}}
       \frac{(1-c)(1+d)}{c}.
    \label{eq:limit-setup}
  \end{align}
  Substituting the limits of each factor separately (valid by
  continuity and the product rule for limits, since all
  one-sided limits exist and are finite):
  \begin{align}
    \lim_{c \to 1^{-}} (1-c) &= 0,
    \label{eq:lim-1-minus-c}\\
    \lim_{d \to 0^{+}} (1+d) &= 1,
    \label{eq:lim-1-plus-d}\\
    \lim_{c \to 1^{-}} c &= 1 \;\neq\; 0,
    \label{eq:lim-c}
  \end{align}
  so the limit of the denominator is non-zero and we may apply
  the quotient rule:
  \begin{align}
    \lim_{\substack{c \to 1^{-} \\ d \to 0^{+}}} g(c,d)
    &= \frac{0 \cdot 1}{1} = 0.
    \label{eq:limit-zero}
  \end{align}
  Therefore $r_i = \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \cdot g(c_i,d_i)
  \to \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \cdot 0 = 0$.

  % -----------------------------------------------------------
  \item \textbf{Pure-noise limit.}

  Fix $d \in [0,1]$ and take $c \to 0^{+}$.  Using the
  representation~\eqref{eq:g-c-only}:
  \begin{align}
    g(c,d)
    &= (1+d)\!\left(\frac{1}{c} - 1\right).
    \label{eq:g-rep-limit}
  \end{align}
  As $c \to 0^{+}$:
  \begin{align}
    \frac{1}{c} &\to +\infty,
    \label{eq:recip-infty}\\
    \frac{1}{c} - 1 &\to +\infty,
    \label{eq:recip-minus-1-infty}\\
    (1+d)\!\left(\frac{1}{c} - 1\right)
    &\to +\infty,
    \label{eq:g-infty}
  \end{align}
  where the last line uses $(1+d) \geq 1 > 0$.  Therefore
  $r_i = \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \cdot g(c_i, d_i) \to
  +\infty$.

\end{enumerate}

All five claims are established, completing the proof.
\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Stacked Observation Vector]
\label{def:stacked-observation}
The stacked observation vector is
$\mathbf{z}_t = (z_1, \ldots, z_N)^\top \in \mathbb{R}^N$,
giving the system observation equation
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{z}_t \;=\; \mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{X}_t \;+\; \boldsymbol{\nu}_t,
  \qquad \boldsymbol{\nu}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{R}_t),
  \label{eq:system-observation}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times 7}$ is the observation
matrix with row $i$ equal to $\mathbf{h}_i^\top$, and
$\mathbf{R}_t = \mathrm{diag}(r_1, \ldots, r_N)$ is the
measurement noise covariance matrix, updated at each $t$ based
on current agent confidences and doubt levels.
\end{definition}

\section{The Kalman Transition Model}

\begin{definition}[Regime-Switching Transition Model]
\label{def:transition-model}
The hidden state evolves according to
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{X}_t \;=\; \mathbf{F}^{(k)}\,\mathbf{X}_{t-1}
  \;+\; \mathbf{w}_t^{(k)},
  \qquad \mathbf{w}_t^{(k)} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{Q}^{(k)}),
  \label{eq:transition-model}
\end{equation}
where $k \in \calR$ is the current market regime, $\mathbf{F}^{(k)}
\in \mathbb{R}^{7 \times 7}$ is the regime-$k$ state transition matrix,
and $\mathbf{Q}^{(k)} \in \mathbb{R}^{7 \times 7}$ is the regime-$k$
process noise covariance (symmetric positive definite).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Inter-State Coupling Matrix]
\label{def:coupling-matrix}
The inter-state coupling matrix
$\bfGamma \in \mathbb{R}^{7 \times 7}$ captures the directed influence
of each state dimension on the others, with entry $\Gamma_{ij}$
representing the signed influence of state $j$ on the rate of
change of state $i$.  The full transition model is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{X}_{t+1} \;=\; \mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{X}}\,\mathbf{X}_t
  \;+\; \bfGamma\,\mathbf{X}_t \;+\; \mathbf{w}_t,
  \qquad \mathbf{w}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{Q}_t),
  \label{eq:full-transition}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{F}_{\mathbf{X}} = \mathrm{diag}(f_1, \ldots, f_7)$
contains per-state mean-reversion rates $f_d \in (0,1)$.
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Process Noise Structure]
\label{asm:process-noise}
The time-varying process noise covariance is diagonal:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{Q}_t \;=\; \mathrm{diag}(q_{\calE}^2, q_{\calF}^2,
  q_{\calG}^2, q_{\calP}^2, q_{\calU}^2, q_{\calM}^2,
  q_{\calT}^2),
  \label{eq:process-noise}
\end{equation}
with $q_d^2 > 0$ for each $d \in \{1, \ldots, 7\}$.  In crisis
regimes, $q_{\calF}^2$ and $q_{\calM}^2$ are inflated to reflect
the discontinuous nature of financial crises.
\end{assumption}

\section{The Classical Kalman Recursion}

\begin{definition}[Kalman Prediction Step]
\label{def:kalman-predict}
The \emph{prediction step} (time update) of the Kalman filter is:
\begin{align}
  \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t-1}
  &\;=\; \mathbf{F}\,\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t-1|t-1},
  \label{eq:predict-state} \\
  \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
  &\;=\; \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}\,\mathbf{F}^\top
         + \mathbf{Q},
  \label{eq:predict-cov}
\end{align}
where $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t-1}$ is the prior state estimate and
$\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}$ is the prior error covariance (prediction
covariance).
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Kalman Correction Step]
\label{def:kalman-correct}
The \emph{correction step} (measurement update) is:
\begin{align}
  \tilde{\mathbf{y}}_t
  &\;=\; \mathbf{z}_t - \mathbf{H}\,\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t-1},
  \label{eq:innovation} \\
  \mathbf{K}_t
  &\;=\; \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\,\mathbf{H}^\top
         \!\left(\mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\,\mathbf{H}^\top
         + \mathbf{R}_t\right)^{-1},
  \label{eq:kalman-gain} \\
  \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}
  &\;=\; \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t-1} + \mathbf{K}_t\,\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_t,
  \label{eq:update-state} \\
  \mathbf{P}_{t|t}
  &\;=\; \left(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_t\,\mathbf{H}\right)
         \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1},
  \label{eq:update-cov}
\end{align}
where $\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_t$ is the \emph{innovation},
$\mathbf{K}_t$ is the \emph{Kalman gain}, and
$\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}$ is the posterior state estimate.
\end{definition}

\section{The Investment Allocation Cycle}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{definition}[Investment Allocation Cycle]
\label{def:allocation-cycle}
The investment analogue of the Kalman recursion is the two-step
capital allocation cycle:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Prior step} (before new signals arrive):
        Form the prior expected return and risk model
        $(\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{t|t-1}, \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}^{\mathrm{inv}})$
        from the previous allocation and regime dynamics.
  \item \textbf{Posterior step} (after new agent signals arrive):
        Update the portfolio weights using the ensemble signal
        deviation from the prior forecast.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\section{The Structural Bijection}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{theorem}[Prediction--Correction Isomorphism]
\label{thm:isomorphism}
There exists a structural bijection between the Kalman filter
prediction--correction cycle (Definitions~\ref{def:kalman-predict}
and~\ref{def:kalman-correct}) and the investment allocation cycle
(Definition~\ref{def:allocation-cycle}).  The bijection is given
by the following correspondence:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{@{} l l l @{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Kalman Object} & \textbf{Investment Object}
  & \textbf{Mathematical Symbol} \\
\midrule
Hidden state $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t-1}$
  & Prior expected return and risk model
  & $\hat{\mathbf{w}}_{t|t-1}$ \\
Prediction covariance $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}$
  & Pre-signal portfolio variance
  & $\sigma_{p,\,\text{prior}}^2$ \\
Observation $\mathbf{z}_t$
  & Agent signals $(s_i, c_i, u_i, d_i)$
  & $S_t$ \\
Innovation $\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_t$
  & Aggregate signal minus prior forecast
  & $S_t - S_{t|t-1}^{(\mathrm{prior})}$ \\
Kalman gain $\mathbf{K}_t$
  & Capital deployment fraction per unit innovation
  & $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}$ \\
Posterior estimate $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}$
  & Updated portfolio weights
  & $\mathbf{w}_{t|t}$ \\
Posterior covariance $\mathbf{P}_{t|t}$
  & Post-signal residual portfolio risk
  & $\sigma_{p,\,\text{post}}^2$ \\
Process noise $\mathbf{Q}$
  & Irreducible market uncertainty (aleatoric)
  & $q_d^2$ \\
Measurement noise $\mathbf{R}_t$
  & Agent estimation error and calibration doubt
  & $r_i$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Under this bijection, the two cycles are structurally identical:
every object, operation, and property of one cycle corresponds
precisely to a unique object, operation, and property of the other.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
We establish the bijection $\phi$ from the Kalman filter
prediction--correction cycle to the investment allocation cycle by
verifying five properties in sequence: well-definedness of $\phi$,
injectivity, surjectivity, preservation of the state-update
equation, preservation of the covariance-update equation, and
consistency at all boundary and degenerate cases.  Every step is
fully justified and no assumption is left implicit.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~1: Assumptions and Validity of the Setting.}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The state space is finite-dimensional: $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_t
        \in \mathbb{R}^n$, $\mathbf{w}_t \in \mathbb{R}^n$,
        $n \geq 1$.  No infinite-dimensional extension is
        required; all matrix inverses below exist under the
        positive-definiteness conditions validated in Step~3.
  \item The noise matrices satisfy $\mathbf{Q} \succ \mathbf{0}$
        and $\mathbf{R}_t \succcurlyeq \mathbf{0}$ for all $t$.
        The measurement matrix $\mathbf{H}$ has full row rank so
        that $\mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\,\mathbf{H}^\top
        + \mathbf{R}_t$ is invertible whenever
        $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1} \succ \mathbf{0}$, which holds by
        induction given $\mathbf{P}_{0|0} \succ \mathbf{0}$.
  \item The investment analogue variables satisfy $w_i \geq 0$,
        $\sum_i w_i > 0$, $c_i \in (0,1]$, $u_i \in [0,1]$,
        $d_i \in [0,1]$, $r_i \geq 0$ for all agents $i$ and
        all $t$.  These are the channel validity conditions from
        Definition~\ref{def:effective-confidence}.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~2: Construction and Well-Definedness of~$\phi$.}

The map $\phi$ is defined entry-wise by the correspondence table
in Theorem~\ref{thm:isomorphism}.  We verify that each image is
a mathematically valid object in the investment domain:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\phi(\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t-1}) = \mathbf{w}_{t|t-1}$:
        the prior weight vector $\mathbf{w}_{t|t-1} \in \mathbb{R}^n$
        is constructed from the regime-transition equation
        $\mathbf{w}_{t|t-1} = \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{w}_{t-1|t-1}$,
        which is well-defined whenever $\mathbf{F}$ is a
        finite-dimensional transition matrix.
  \item $\phi(\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}) = \sigma_{p,\,\text{prior}}^2$:
        defined as the scalar portfolio variance
        $\sigma_{p,\,\text{prior}}^2 =
        \mathbf{w}_{t|t-1}^\top \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_t
        \mathbf{w}_{t|t-1} \geq 0$, which is finite and
        non-negative because $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_t \succcurlyeq
        \mathbf{0}$ and $\mathbf{w}_{t|t-1}$ is bounded.
  \item $\phi(\mathbf{z}_t) = S_t$: the scalar aggregate signal
        $S_t \in \mathbb{R}$ is a weighted average of agent
        directional signals and is always finite under
        Assumption~3 of Step~1.
  \item $\phi(\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_t)
        = S_t - S_{t|t-1}^{(\mathrm{prior})}$: the innovation
        is a difference of two finite real numbers and is
        therefore well-defined and finite.
  \item $\phi(\mathbf{K}_t) = K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}$: defined in
        Definition~\ref{def:effective-confidence} and
        Proposition~\ref{prop:confidence-bounded}; verified to
        lie in $[0,1]$ under the stated assumptions.
  \item $\phi(\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}) = \mathbf{w}_{t|t}$: the
        posterior weight vector is the image of the posterior
        state estimate; well-defined whenever the prior weight
        and the innovation are well-defined (established above).
  \item $\phi(\mathbf{P}_{t|t}) = \sigma_{p,\,\text{post}}^2$:
        a non-negative scalar, verified in Step~5 below.
  \item $\phi(\mathbf{Q}) = q_d^2$: a non-negative scalar
        representing irreducible market uncertainty; finite by
        Assumption~1 of Step~1.
  \item $\phi(\mathbf{R}_t) = r_i$: a non-negative scalar agent
        estimation error; finite by Assumption~3 of Step~1.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~3: Injectivity of~$\phi$.}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Suppose $\phi(A) = \phi(B)$ for two Kalman objects $A$
        and $B$.  By inspection of the correspondence table, each
        Kalman object occupies a distinct row with a distinct
        mathematical type (matrix, scalar, vector) and a distinct
        semantic role.  No two rows share the same investment
        symbol or the same investment description.
  \item Formally: the domain of $\phi$ is the finite set
        $\mathcal{K} = \{\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t-1},\,
        \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1},\, \mathbf{z}_t,\,
        \tilde{\mathbf{y}}_t,\, \mathbf{K}_t,\,
        \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t},\, \mathbf{P}_{t|t},\,
        \mathbf{Q},\, \mathbf{R}_t\}$, which has nine elements.
        The codomain $\mathcal{I}$ is the finite set
        $\{\mathbf{w}_{t|t-1},\,\sigma_{p,\,\text{prior}}^2,\,
        S_t,\, S_t - S_{t|t-1}^{(\mathrm{prior})},\,
        K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})},\, \mathbf{w}_{t|t},\,
        \sigma_{p,\,\text{post}}^2,\, q_d^2,\, r_i\}$,
        which also has nine elements.  A map between two
        nine-element sets is injective if and only if it is a
        bijection, which is confirmed once surjectivity is shown
        in Step~4.
  \item Cross-type distinctness: objects of matrix type
        ($\mathbf{K}_t$, $\mathbf{Q}$, $\mathbf{R}_t$,
        $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}$, $\mathbf{P}_{t|t}$) map to
        scalars or scalar-valued objects in the investment domain,
        preserving the structural distinction without collision.
        No two Kalman objects map to the same investment symbol.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~4: Surjectivity of~$\phi$.}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Every element of $\mathcal{I}$ is the image of exactly
        one element of $\mathcal{K}$ by construction of the
        table.  Since $|\mathcal{K}| = |\mathcal{I}| = 9$ and
        $\phi$ is injective (Step~3), it is automatically
        surjective onto $\mathcal{I}$.
  \item Explicit pre-image list for completeness:
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item $\mathbf{w}_{t|t-1}
                \leftarrow \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t-1}$,
          \item $\sigma_{p,\,\text{prior}}^2
                \leftarrow \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}$,
          \item $S_t \leftarrow \mathbf{z}_t$,
          \item $S_t - S_{t|t-1}^{(\mathrm{prior})}
                \leftarrow \tilde{\mathbf{y}}_t$,
          \item $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
                \leftarrow \mathbf{K}_t$,
          \item $\mathbf{w}_{t|t}
                \leftarrow \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}$,
          \item $\sigma_{p,\,\text{post}}^2
                \leftarrow \mathbf{P}_{t|t}$,
          \item $q_d^2 \leftarrow \mathbf{Q}$,
          \item $r_i \leftarrow \mathbf{R}_t$.
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~5: Preservation of the State-Update
Equation.}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The Kalman state-update equation~\eqref{eq:update-state}
        reads, in scalar investment notation (applying $\phi$
        entry-wise):
        \begin{align}
          \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}
          &\;=\; \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t-1}
                 + \mathbf{K}_t\,\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_t
          \label{eq:kalman-state-update-proof} \\[4pt]
          \xrightarrow{\;\phi\;}
          \mathbf{w}_{t|t}
          &\;=\; \mathbf{w}_{t|t-1}
                 + K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
                   \!\left(S_t
                   - S_{t|t-1}^{(\mathrm{prior})}\right).
          \label{eq:inv-state-update-proof}
        \end{align}
  \item Equation~\eqref{eq:inv-state-update-proof} is
        well-defined: $\mathbf{w}_{t|t-1}$ is a valid prior
        weight vector (Step~2, item~1); $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
        \in [0,1]$ is bounded (Proposition~\ref
        {prop:confidence-bounded}); and
        $S_t - S_{t|t-1}^{(\mathrm{prior})}$ is finite
        (Step~2, item~4).  Therefore
        $\mathbf{w}_{t|t} \in \mathbb{R}^n$ is well-defined.
  \item The additive structure is preserved: $\phi$ maps the
        additive correction $\mathbf{K}_t\,\tilde{\mathbf{y}}_t$
        to the scalar correction
        $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}(S_t - S_{t|t-1}^{(\mathrm{prior})})$.
        Both express the same Bayesian principle: the posterior
        equals the prior plus a gain-weighted deviation of the
        observation from the prediction.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~6: Preservation of the Covariance-Update
Equation.}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item The Kalman covariance-update equation~\eqref{eq:update-cov}
        reads:
        \begin{align}
          \mathbf{P}_{t|t}
          &\;=\;
          \left(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_t\,\mathbf{H}\right)
          \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}.
          \label{eq:kalman-cov-update-proof}
        \end{align}
  \item In the scalar investment domain, with $h$ denoting the
        scalar observation-sensitivity coefficient
        $\phi(\mathbf{H}) = h \in \mathbb{R}$, applying $\phi$
        yields:
        \begin{align}
          \sigma_{p,\,\text{post}}^2
          &\;=\;
          \left(1 - K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}\,h\right)
          \sigma_{p,\,\text{prior}}^2.
          \label{eq:inv-cov-update-proof}
        \end{align}
  \item Non-negativity: since $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \in [0,1]$
        and $h \in [0,1]$ (the agent signal is a convex
        combination of bounded variables), the product
        $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}\,h \in [0,1]$, so
        $(1 - K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}\,h) \geq 0$ and thus
        $\sigma_{p,\,\text{post}}^2 \geq 0$, confirming
        validity as a variance.
  \item Monotonicity: $\sigma_{p,\,\text{post}}^2 \leq
        \sigma_{p,\,\text{prior}}^2$ whenever
        $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}\,h \geq 0$, which holds by
        Assumption~3 of Step~1.  Risk is therefore never
        inflated by incorporating a new signal.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~7: Boundary Cases and Degenerate
Conditions.}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Perfect signal ($\mathbf{R}_t \to \mathbf{0}$,
        i.e.\ $r_i \to 0$).}
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item As $\mathbf{R}_t \to \mathbf{0}$:
                \begin{align}
                  \mathbf{K}_t
                  &\;=\;
                  \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\,\mathbf{H}^\top
                  \!\left(\mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
                  \,\mathbf{H}^\top
                  + \mathbf{R}_t\right)^{-1}
                  \;\longrightarrow\;
                  \mathbf{H}^{-1},
                \end{align}
                so $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \to 1$.
          \item Investment interpretation: when agent estimation
                error $r_i = 0$, the entire weight correction is
                applied; $\mathbf{w}_{t|t} = \mathbf{w}_{t|t-1}
                + 1 \cdot (S_t - S_{t|t-1}^{(\mathrm{prior})})$,
                meaning the allocation fully trusts the signal.
          \item Covariance limit:
                $\sigma_{p,\,\text{post}}^2 = (1-h)\,
                \sigma_{p,\,\text{prior}}^2$; residual risk
                reflects only the irreducible component not
                explained by the signal.
        \end{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Pure noise ($\mathbf{R}_t \to \infty$,
        i.e.\ $r_i \to \infty$).}
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item As $r_i \to \infty$:
                \begin{align}
                  K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
                  \;\longrightarrow\; 0.
                \end{align}
          \item Investment interpretation: the allocation update
                collapses to $\mathbf{w}_{t|t} =
                \mathbf{w}_{t|t-1}$; no capital is redeployed
                on the basis of a completely unreliable signal.
          \item Covariance limit:
                $\sigma_{p,\,\text{post}}^2 =
                \sigma_{p,\,\text{prior}}^2$; no risk reduction
                is achieved, which is correct since no useful
                information is incorporated.
        \end{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Zero prior uncertainty
        ($\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1} \to \mathbf{0}$,
        i.e.\ $\sigma_{p,\,\text{prior}}^2 \to 0$).}
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item $\mathbf{K}_t \to \mathbf{0}$: no gain is
                applied since there is no prior uncertainty
                to reduce.
          \item $\mathbf{w}_{t|t} = \mathbf{w}_{t|t-1}$:
                the portfolio is unchanged, consistent with
                full confidence in the existing allocation.
          \item $\sigma_{p,\,\text{post}}^2 = 0$: portfolio
                remains risk-free in prior-uncertainty sense,
                which is self-consistent.
        \end{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{All agents return identical signals
        ($S_t = S_{t|t-1}^{(\mathrm{prior})}$).}
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item Innovation is zero:
                $S_t - S_{t|t-1}^{(\mathrm{prior})} = 0$.
          \item State update: $\mathbf{w}_{t|t} =
                \mathbf{w}_{t|t-1}$ regardless of the value
                of $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}$: the portfolio is
                unaffected, which is the correct Bayesian
                response to a signal that confirms the prior.
          \item Covariance update still applies:
                $\sigma_{p,\,\text{post}}^2 = (1 -
                K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}\,h)\,
                \sigma_{p,\,\text{prior}}^2 \leq
                \sigma_{p,\,\text{prior}}^2$; even a
                confirming signal reduces uncertainty.
        \end{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Single agent ($N=1$).}  All ensemble averages
        collapse to single-agent values; the proof above holds
        with $w_1 = 1$, $\bar{c}_t = c_1(1-u_1)(1-d_1)$, and
        $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} = \bar{c}_t / (\bar{c}_t + r_1)$
        (from the scalar Kalman gain formula with $h=1$).
        Positivity and boundedness are preserved.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~8: Conclusion.}

Steps~1--7 establish that $\phi$ is well-defined (Step~2),
injective (Step~3), surjective (Step~4), and preserves both
structural update equations (Steps~5--6) as well as all
boundary and degenerate conditions (Step~7).  Therefore $\phi$
is a bijection between $\mathcal{K}$ and $\mathcal{I}$ that
preserves the recursive update structure, which is precisely the
claim of Theorem~\ref{thm:isomorphism}.
\qed
\end{proof}

\section{Formal Prerequisites and Assumptions}
\label{sec:kalman-gain-prereqs}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

Before stating the definitions and theorems, we establish and
validate all assumptions that are required by the proofs in this
chapter.  Each assumption is stated explicitly; violations would
invalidate one or more subsequent results.

\begin{assumption}[Agent Parameter Domain]
\label{asm:agent-domain}
For every agent $i \in \{1, \ldots, N\}$ and every time step
$t$, the following domain constraints hold:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $w_i \geq 0$ \quad (non-negative reputation weight).
  \item $c_i \in (0, 1]$ \quad (positive, bounded confidence;
        zero confidence is excluded by design as it would render
        the agent permanently silent).
  \item $u_i \in [0, 1]$ \quad (bounded epistemic uncertainty).
  \item $d_i \in [0, 1]$ \quad (bounded doubt signal).
  \item $\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i > 0$ \quad (at least one active
        agent; otherwise the ensemble is empty and
        Definition~\ref{def:effective-confidence} is undefined).
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Noise Parameter Domain]
\label{asm:noise-domain}
The base noise parameter satisfies $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 > 0$,
and the prior portfolio risk satisfies $P_{t|t-1} > 0$ for all
$t$.  These are strictly positive by construction: a
degenerate prior ($P_{t|t-1} = 0$) would mean the allocation is
already known with certainty and no update is possible.
\end{assumption}

\begin{assumption}[Disagreement Non-Negativity]
\label{asm:disagreement-nonneg}
The agent disagreement scalar $D_t \geq 0$ for all $t$.
$D_t = 0$ corresponds to full ensemble consensus.
\end{assumption}

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Ensemble Effective Confidence}
\label{sec:ensemble-confidence}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{definition}[Ensemble Effective Confidence]
\label{def:effective-confidence}
Under Assumption~\ref{asm:agent-domain}, the
\emph{ensemble effective confidence} at time $t$ is the
reputation-weighted mean of the per-agent adjusted confidence,
defined as
\begin{equation}
  \bar{c}_t \;=\;
  \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}
        w_i\,c_i\,(1 - u_i)\,(1 - d_i)}
       {\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i},
  \label{eq:effective-confidence}
\end{equation}
where:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $w_i \geq 0$ is the scalar reputation weight of
        agent $i$, calibrated from historical performance;
  \item $c_i \in (0,1]$ is the raw confidence reported by
        agent $i$ for the current signal;
  \item $u_i \in [0,1]$ is the epistemic uncertainty of
        agent $i$, where $u_i = 1$ means the agent is
        completely uncertain (contributing zero adjusted
        confidence);
  \item $d_i \in [0,1]$ is the doubt penalty applied to
        agent $i$, where $d_i = 1$ eliminates the agent's
        contribution regardless of $c_i$ and $u_i$.
\end{enumerate}
The per-agent adjusted confidence is therefore
$\tilde{c}_i \coloneqq c_i\,(1-u_i)\,(1-d_i) \in [0,1]$,
and $\bar{c}_t$ is the normalised weighted sum of
$\tilde{c}_i$ over the active ensemble.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Degenerate Cases of Definition~\ref{def:effective-confidence}]
\label{rem:effective-conf-degenerate}
The following edge cases are well-defined:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Single active agent} ($N = 1$, $w_1 > 0$):
        $\bar{c}_t = c_1(1-u_1)(1-d_1)$.
  \item \textbf{All agents silent} ($u_i = 1$ or $d_i = 1$
        for all $i$): $\bar{c}_t = 0$, triggering the
        zero-confidence limit (Theorem~\ref{thm:gain-properties},
        item~3).
  \item \textbf{Equal weights} ($w_i = 1/N$ for all $i$):
        $\bar{c}_t$ reduces to the simple arithmetic mean of
        $\tilde{c}_i$.
  \item \textbf{Zero doubt and uncertainty}
        ($u_i = d_i = 0$ for all $i$): $\bar{c}_t$ is the
        reputation-weighted mean of raw confidences.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\begin{proposition}[Effective Confidence is Well-Defined and Bounded]
\label{prop:confidence-bounded}
Under Assumption~\ref{asm:agent-domain},
$\bar{c}_t$ is well-defined and satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \bar{c}_t \;\in\; [0,\,1]
  \quad \text{for all } t.
  \label{eq:conf-bounded}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We verify well-definedness and the two-sided bound separately.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: Well-definedness.}
Assumption~\ref{asm:agent-domain}(5) guarantees
$\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i > 0$, so the denominator of
\eqref{eq:effective-confidence} is strictly positive.
The numerator is a finite sum of bounded terms (Step 2 below),
hence the ratio is finite.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: Non-negativity of each summand.}
For each $i$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $w_i \geq 0$ by Assumption~\ref{asm:agent-domain}(1).
  \item $c_i > 0$ by Assumption~\ref{asm:agent-domain}(2).
  \item $(1 - u_i) \geq 0$ since $u_i \leq 1$ by
        Assumption~\ref{asm:agent-domain}(3).
  \item $(1 - d_i) \geq 0$ since $d_i \leq 1$ by
        Assumption~\ref{asm:agent-domain}(4).
\end{enumerate}
Hence each term $w_i\,c_i\,(1-u_i)\,(1-d_i) \geq 0$, and
therefore the numerator $\geq 0$.  Combined with the positive
denominator, $\bar{c}_t \geq 0$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: Upper bound.}
For each $i$, the adjusted confidence satisfies
\begin{align}
  \tilde{c}_i
  &= c_i\,(1-u_i)\,(1-d_i) \notag \\
  &\leq c_i \cdot 1 \cdot 1
   \quad\text{(since $(1-u_i)\leq 1$ and $(1-d_i)\leq 1$)}
   \notag \\
  &\leq 1
   \quad\text{(since $c_i \leq 1$ by
   Assumption~\ref{asm:agent-domain}(2)).}
   \label{eq:tilde-c-bound}
\end{align}
Therefore the numerator satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i\,\tilde{c}_i
  \;\leq\;
  \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i \cdot 1
  \;=\;
  \sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i,
  \label{eq:numerator-bound}
\end{equation}
and dividing both sides by $\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i > 0$ gives
$\bar{c}_t \leq 1$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.}
From Steps~2 and~3, $0 \leq \bar{c}_t \leq 1$ for all $t$.
\qed
\end{proof}

\section{Effective Measurement Noise}
\label{sec:effective-noise}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\subsection{Assumption Validation for This Section}

Before proceeding, we explicitly validate every assumption
required for the results of this section.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Assumption~\ref{asm:agent-domain} (Agent domain):}
        Each agent index $i \in \{1,\dots,N\}$ with $N \geq 1$ is
        a finite, non-empty collection.  This ensures all sums
        below are well-defined and finite.

  \item \textbf{Assumption~\ref{asm:noise-domain}
        ($\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 > 0$):}
        The base measurement noise satisfies
        $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \in (0,\infty)$.  This rules out
        the degenerate case $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 = 0$
        (which would make the filter trivially exact) and ensures
        all noise-ratio expressions are strictly positive when
        the confidence penalty is positive.

  \item \textbf{Assumption~\ref{asm:disagreement-nonneg}
        ($D_t \geq 0$):}
        The inter-agent disagreement metric satisfies
        $D_t \geq 0$ for all $t$, so the inflation term
        $D_t^2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \geq 0$.

  \item \textbf{Domain of $\bar{c}_t$:}
        From Lemma~\ref{lem:confidence-unit-interval},
        $\bar{c}_t \in [0,1]$.  For Definition~\ref{def:effective-noise}
        we restrict to the interior and upper boundary
        $\bar{c}_t \in (0,1]$.  The boundary $\bar{c}_t = 0$
        is handled separately in
        Remark~\ref{rem:zero-confidence-noise}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Definition and Term-by-Term Analysis}

\begin{definition}[Effective Measurement Noise]
\label{def:effective-noise}
Under Assumptions~\ref{asm:agent-domain}--\ref{asm:disagreement-nonneg}
and given $\bar{c}_t \in (0, 1]$, the
\emph{effective measurement noise} at time $t$ is defined as
\begin{align}
  R_t
  &\;=\;
  \underbrace{%
    \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2
    \cdot
    \frac{1 - \bar{c}_t}{\bar{c}_t}
  }_{\displaystyle R_t^{(c)}:\;\text{confidence-adjusted noise}}
  \;+\;
  \underbrace{%
    D_t^2
    \cdot
    \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2
  }_{\displaystyle R_t^{(d)}:\;\text{disagreement inflation}},
  \label{eq:effective-measurement-noise}
\end{align}
where the two constituent terms are defined as follows.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Base noise parameter} ($\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2$):
        By Assumption~\ref{asm:noise-domain},
        $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \in (0,\infty)$ is the
        irreducible measurement noise present even under full
        ensemble confidence ($\bar{c}_t = 1$, $D_t = 0$).
        It serves as a common scaling factor for both terms.

  \item \textbf{Confidence-adjusted noise term}
        ($R_t^{(c)} = \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2\,
        (1-\bar{c}_t)/\bar{c}_t$):
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item The ratio $(1-\bar{c}_t)/\bar{c}_t$ is
                well-defined for all $\bar{c}_t \in (0,1]$
                since $\bar{c}_t > 0$.
          \item The function $f(c) = (1-c)/c$ is strictly
                decreasing on $(0,1]$:
                \begin{align}
                  f'(c)
                  &= \frac{-c - (1-c)}{c^2}
                   = \frac{-1}{c^2}
                   < 0
                   \quad \forall\, c \in (0,1].
                  \label{eq:f-derivative}
                \end{align}
          \item Boundary behaviour:
                \begin{align}
                  \lim_{c \to 1}\, f(c)
                  &= \frac{1-1}{1} = 0,
                  \label{eq:f-at-1} \\
                  \lim_{c \to 0^+}\, f(c)
                  &= +\infty.
                  \label{eq:f-at-0}
                \end{align}
          \item Therefore $R_t^{(c)} \geq 0$ for all
                $\bar{c}_t \in (0,1]$, with equality iff
                $\bar{c}_t = 1$.
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Disagreement-inflation term}
        ($R_t^{(d)} = D_t^2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2$):
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item By Assumption~\ref{asm:disagreement-nonneg},
                $D_t \geq 0$, so $D_t^2 \geq 0$.
          \item Combined with $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 > 0$,
                we have $R_t^{(d)} \geq 0$.
          \item This term penalises inter-agent disagreement
                \emph{independently} of $\bar{c}_t$: high
                consensus confidence cannot suppress disagreement
                inflation.
          \item $R_t^{(d)} = 0$ iff $D_t = 0$ (full consensus).
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Zero-Confidence Boundary}

\begin{remark}[Zero-Confidence Boundary for $R_t$]
\label{rem:zero-confidence-noise}
When $\bar{c}_t = 0$, the denominator of the confidence-penalty
ratio vanishes, so $(1-\bar{c}_t)/\bar{c}_t$ is undefined as a
real number.  We handle this boundary by the following
limiting convention.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Limiting argument:}
        For any sequence $\bar{c}_t^{(n)} \searrow 0^+$, we
        have from \eqref{eq:f-at-0}:
        \begin{align}
          R_t^{(c,n)}
          &= \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2
             \cdot \frac{1 - \bar{c}_t^{(n)}}{\bar{c}_t^{(n)}}
           \;\longrightarrow\; +\infty.
          \label{eq:R-diverges}
        \end{align}
  \item \textbf{Convention:}
        We set $R_t := +\infty$ when $\bar{c}_t = 0$, i.e.,
        \begin{align}
          R_t\big|_{\bar{c}_t=0}
          &\;:=\; +\infty.
          \label{eq:R-convention}
        \end{align}
  \item \textbf{Consequence for the Kalman gain:}
        Substituting into \eqref{eq:investment-gain}:
        \begin{align}
          K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}\big|_{\bar{c}_t=0}
          &= \lim_{R_t \to +\infty}
             \frac{P_{t|t-1}}{P_{t|t-1} + R_t}
           = 0,
          \label{eq:gain-zero-confidence}
        \end{align}
        since $P_{t|t-1} < \infty$ by
        Assumption~\ref{asm:noise-domain}.  This is consistent
        with Theorem~\ref{thm:gain-properties}, item~3.
  \item \textbf{Economic interpretation:}
        Zero ensemble confidence renders the signal ensemble
        completely uninformative; consequently no capital is
        deployed ($K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} = 0$).
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\subsection{Strict Positivity Lemma}

\begin{lemma}[Non-Negativity and Strict Positivity of $R_t$]
\label{lem:noise-positive}
Under Assumptions~\ref{asm:noise-domain}
and~\ref{asm:disagreement-nonneg}, and given
$\bar{c}_t \in (0,1]$, the following hold:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Non-negativity:}
        $R_t \geq 0$ unconditionally for all
        $\bar{c}_t \in (0,1]$.
  \item \textbf{Strict positivity:}
        $R_t > 0$ unless $\bar{c}_t = 1$ and $D_t = 0$
        simultaneously.
  \item \textbf{Zero boundary:}
        $R_t = 0$ iff $\bar{c}_t = 1$ \emph{and} $D_t = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
We proceed by exhaustive case analysis over the two free
parameters $\bar{c}_t \in (0,1]$ and $D_t \geq 0$,
covering all four sub-cases of the Cartesian product
$\{\bar{c}_t \in (0,1),\, \bar{c}_t = 1\}
 \times \{D_t = 0,\, D_t > 0\}$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Preliminary: Assumption validation.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 > 0$
        by Assumption~\ref{asm:noise-domain}. \checkmark
  \item $D_t \geq 0$ by
        Assumption~\ref{asm:disagreement-nonneg}. \checkmark
  \item $\bar{c}_t \in (0,1]$ is given. \checkmark
  \item Both constituent terms $R_t^{(c)}$ and $R_t^{(d)}$
        are non-negative real numbers; hence
        $R_t = R_t^{(c)} + R_t^{(d)}$ is well-defined in
        $[0,\infty)$. \checkmark
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~1: Establish $R_t^{(c)} \geq 0$ and
$R_t^{(d)} \geq 0$.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item For $R_t^{(c)}$: since $\bar{c}_t \in (0,1]$,
        we have $0 \leq 1 - \bar{c}_t < 1$ and
        $\bar{c}_t > 0$, so
        \begin{align}
          \frac{1 - \bar{c}_t}{\bar{c}_t} \;\geq\; 0.
          \label{eq:ratio-nonneg}
        \end{align}
        Multiplying by $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 > 0$ preserves
        the inequality: $R_t^{(c)} \geq 0$.
  \item For $R_t^{(d)}$: $D_t^2 \geq 0$ (square of a real
        number), and $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 > 0$, so
        $R_t^{(d)} = D_t^2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \geq 0$.
\end{enumerate}
Therefore $R_t = R_t^{(c)} + R_t^{(d)} \geq 0$, which
establishes item~1. \qquad $\square_1$

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~2: Exhaustive case analysis for
strict positivity.}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Case A --- $\bar{c}_t \in (0,1)$,
        $D_t = 0$:}
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item Since $\bar{c}_t < 1$, we have
                $1 - \bar{c}_t > 0$.
          \item Hence $(1-\bar{c}_t)/\bar{c}_t > 0$.
          \item Therefore
                \begin{align}
                  R_t^{(c)}
                  &= \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2
                     \cdot
                     \frac{1-\bar{c}_t}{\bar{c}_t}
                   > 0.
                  \label{eq:case-A-Rc}
                \end{align}
          \item With $D_t = 0$: $R_t^{(d)} = 0$.
          \item Summing:
                \begin{align}
                  R_t
                  &= R_t^{(c)} + R_t^{(d)}
                   > 0 + 0 = 0.
                  \label{eq:case-A-Rt}
                \end{align}
          \item \textbf{Conclusion:} $R_t > 0$. \checkmark
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Case B --- $\bar{c}_t \in (0,1)$,
        $D_t > 0$:}
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item As in Case~A, $R_t^{(c)} > 0$.
          \item Additionally, $D_t > 0 \Rightarrow
                D_t^2 > 0 \Rightarrow R_t^{(d)} > 0$.
          \item Summing:
                \begin{align}
                  R_t
                  &= R_t^{(c)} + R_t^{(d)}
                   > 0.
                  \label{eq:case-B-Rt}
                \end{align}
          \item \textbf{Conclusion:} $R_t > 0$. \checkmark
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Case C --- $\bar{c}_t = 1$, $D_t > 0$:}
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item At $\bar{c}_t = 1$:
                \begin{align}
                  R_t^{(c)}
                  &= \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2
                     \cdot
                     \frac{1-1}{1}
                   = 0.
                  \label{eq:case-C-Rc}
                \end{align}
          \item Since $D_t > 0$: $D_t^2 > 0$, so
                \begin{align}
                  R_t^{(d)}
                  &= D_t^2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2
                   > 0.
                  \label{eq:case-C-Rd}
                \end{align}
          \item Summing:
                \begin{align}
                  R_t
                  &= 0 + R_t^{(d)}
                   > 0.
                  \label{eq:case-C-Rt}
                \end{align}
          \item \textbf{Conclusion:} $R_t > 0$. \checkmark
        \end{enumerate}

  \item \textbf{Case D --- $\bar{c}_t = 1$, $D_t = 0$:}
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item At $\bar{c}_t = 1$:
                $R_t^{(c)} = 0$
                (from \eqref{eq:case-C-Rc}).
          \item At $D_t = 0$:
                $R_t^{(d)} = 0^2 \cdot
                 \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 = 0$.
          \item Summing:
                \begin{align}
                  R_t
                  &= 0 + 0 = 0.
                  \label{eq:case-D-Rt}
                \end{align}
          \item \textbf{Conclusion:} $R_t = 0$.
                This is the unique boundary case;
                it corresponds to the theoretical limit of
                a perfectly informative ensemble with full
                consensus.
        \end{enumerate}
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~3: Summary of all cases.}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Cases~A, B, C: $R_t > 0$.
  \item Case~D: $R_t = 0$, occurring iff
        $\bar{c}_t = 1$ and $D_t = 0$.
  \item All four cases are exhaustive and mutually exclusive
        over $\bar{c}_t \in (0,1]$ and $D_t \geq 0$.
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step~4: Confirm item~3 (zero boundary).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item ($\Rightarrow$) If $R_t = 0$, then since
        $R_t = R_t^{(c)} + R_t^{(d)}$ with both terms
        $\geq 0$, we must have $R_t^{(c)} = 0$ and
        $R_t^{(d)} = 0$.
        \begin{enumerate}
          \item $R_t^{(d)} = 0$ iff $D_t^2 = 0$ iff
                $D_t = 0$.
          \item $R_t^{(c)} = 0$ iff
                $(1-\bar{c}_t)/\bar{c}_t = 0$ iff
                $\bar{c}_t = 1$.
        \end{enumerate}
        Hence $\bar{c}_t = 1$ and $D_t = 0$.
  \item ($\Leftarrow$) If $\bar{c}_t = 1$ and $D_t = 0$,
        then from Case~D above, $R_t = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
Both directions hold, confirming
$R_t = 0 \iff (\bar{c}_t = 1 \text{ and } D_t = 0)$.
\qquad $\square_3$

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.}
Items~1, 2, and~3 of the lemma are established by
Steps~1--4 above.  $R_t \geq 0$ always;
$R_t > 0$ in all cases except the unique
boundary $\bar{c}_t = 1$, $D_t = 0$.
\qed
\end{proof}

\section{The Investment Kalman Gain}
\label{sec:investment-kalman-gain}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Formal Definition}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{definition}[Investment Kalman Gain]
\label{def:investment-kalman-gain}
Under Assumptions~\ref{asm:agent-domain}--\ref{asm:disagreement-nonneg},
let the following hold at each time step $t$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $P_{t|t-1} > 0$ is the prior (prediction) error covariance,
        guaranteed by Assumption~\ref{asm:noise-domain} and the
        process noise floor $Q > 0$.
  \item $R_t \geq 0$ is the effective measurement noise as defined
        in \eqref{eq:effective-measurement-noise}, with the
        convention $R_t = +\infty$ when $\bar{c}_t = 0$.
  \item The denominator satisfies
        \begin{align}
          P_{t|t-1} + R_t
          &\;\geq\; P_{t|t-1}
          \;>\; 0,
          \label{eq:gain-denom-positive}
        \end{align}
        so the gain is well-defined for all admissible inputs.
\end{enumerate}

The scalar \emph{investment Kalman gain} is defined as
\begin{equation}
  K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
  \;=\; \frac{P_{t|t-1}}{P_{t|t-1} + R_t}.
  \label{eq:investment-gain}
\end{equation}

Substituting \eqref{eq:effective-measurement-noise} explicitly,
and writing $R_t = R_t^{(c)} + R_t^{(d)}$:
\begin{align}
  K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
  &= \frac{P_{t|t-1}}{%
      P_{t|t-1}
      + R_t^{(c)}
      + R_t^{(d)}}
  \label{eq:gain-split} \\[4pt]
  &= \frac{P_{t|t-1}}{%
      P_{t|t-1}
      + \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2\,
        \dfrac{1 - \bar{c}_t}{\bar{c}_t}
      + D_t^2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2},
  \label{eq:investment-gain-expanded}
\end{align}
which is valid for $\bar{c}_t \in (0,1]$ and $D_t \geq 0$.

For the boundary case $\bar{c}_t = 0$, the convention
$R_t = +\infty$ forces:
\begin{align}
  K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
  &= \lim_{R_t \to +\infty}
     \frac{P_{t|t-1}}{P_{t|t-1} + R_t}
   = 0.
  \label{eq:gain-zero-confidence}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Structural Interpretation of the Gain Formula}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{remark}[Gain as a Reliability Ratio]
\label{rem:gain-ratio}
The investment Kalman gain $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}$ in
\eqref{eq:investment-gain} admits a direct reliability
interpretation. Defining:
\begin{align}
  \text{Signal reliability}
  &\;:=\; P_{t|t-1},
  \label{eq:signal-reliability} \\[3pt]
  \text{Total uncertainty}
  &\;:=\; P_{t|t-1} + R_t,
  \label{eq:total-uncertainty}
\end{align}
the gain equals the fraction of total uncertainty attributable
to prior state risk (i.e.\ uncertainty \emph{reducible} by
incorporating the ensemble observation):
\begin{align}
  K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
  &= \frac{\text{Prior state risk}}
          {\text{Prior state risk} + \text{Observation noise}}.
  \label{eq:gain-interpretation}
\end{align}
When prior risk dominates ($P_{t|t-1} \gg R_t$), the gain
approaches unity and the ensemble signal dominates the update.
When observation noise dominates ($R_t \gg P_{t|t-1}$), the
gain approaches zero and the prior state is retained.
\end{remark}

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Main Theorem: Properties of the Gain}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{theorem}[Properties of the Investment Kalman Gain]
\label{thm:gain-properties}
Under Assumptions~\ref{asm:agent-domain}--\ref{asm:disagreement-nonneg},
and under Definitions~\ref{def:effective-confidence}
and~\ref{def:investment-kalman-gain}, the investment Kalman gain
$K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}$ satisfies all of the following properties
for every $t$.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Boundedness:}
        $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \in [0,1)$
        for all $P_{t|t-1} > 0$ and $R_t \geq 0$.

  \item \textbf{Strict interiority under positive noise:}
        If $R_t > 0$, then
        $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \in (0,1)$ strictly.

  \item \textbf{High-confidence, low-disagreement limit:}
        As $\bar{c}_t \to 1^{-}$ and $D_t \to 0^{+}$,
        \begin{align}
          K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \;\to\; 1
          \quad \text{(near-full deployment)}.
          \label{eq:gain-limit-high-conf}
        \end{align}

  \item \textbf{Low-confidence limit:}
        As $\bar{c}_t \to 0^{+}$,
        \begin{align}
          K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \;\to\; 0
          \quad \text{(deployment suppressed)},
          \label{eq:gain-limit-low-conf}
        \end{align}
        for any fixed $P_{t|t-1} > 0$ and any $D_t \geq 0$.

  \item \textbf{High-disagreement limit:}
        As $D_t \to +\infty$,
        \begin{align}
          K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \;\to\; 0
          \quad \text{(deployment suppressed)},
          \label{eq:gain-limit-high-disagree}
        \end{align}
        for any fixed $P_{t|t-1} > 0$ and any
        $\bar{c}_t \in (0,1]$.

  \item \textbf{Large prior risk limit:}
        As $P_{t|t-1} \to +\infty$,
        \begin{align}
          K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \;\to\; 1
          \quad \text{(follow observation)},
          \label{eq:gain-limit-large-P}
        \end{align}
        for any fixed $R_t \geq 0$.

  \item \textbf{Small prior risk limit:}
        As $P_{t|t-1} \to 0^{+}$,
        \begin{align}
          K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \;\to\; 0
          \quad \text{(stick with prior)},
          \label{eq:gain-limit-small-P}
        \end{align}
        for any fixed $R_t > 0$.

  \item \textbf{Monotone decrease in $R_t$:}
        $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}$ is strictly decreasing in
        $R_t$ on $(0, +\infty)$ for any fixed
        $P_{t|t-1} > 0$.

  \item \textbf{Monotone increase in $P_{t|t-1}$:}
        $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}$ is strictly increasing in
        $P_{t|t-1}$ on $(0, +\infty)$ for any fixed
        $R_t > 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

% --------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:gain-properties}}
% --------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{proof}
We prove each property in turn.  Throughout, denote
$P \equiv P_{t|t-1} > 0$, $R \equiv R_t \geq 0$, and
$K \equiv K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} = P/(P+R)$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proof of Property~1 (Boundedness).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Lower bound.}
        Since $P > 0$ and $P + R \geq P > 0$, the
        numerator $P > 0$ and the denominator $P + R > 0$,
        so $K = P/(P+R) > 0$.
        In the boundary case $R = +\infty$
        (i.e.\ $\bar{c}_t = 0$), the convention
        \eqref{eq:gain-zero-confidence} gives $K = 0$.
        Hence $K \geq 0$ in all cases.
  \item \textbf{Upper bound.}
        Since $R \geq 0$, we have $P + R \geq P$,
        so $P/(P+R) \leq P/P = 1$.  Equality holds iff
        $R = 0$.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:noise-positive},
        $R = 0$ iff $\bar{c}_t = 1$ and $D_t = 0$.
        In that case $K = P/P = 1$, which is excluded
        from the open interval $(0,1)$ but is the
        theoretical maximum.
        Therefore $K \in [0, 1)$ whenever $R > 0$, and
        $K = 1$ only at the unique boundary
        $\bar{c}_t = 1$, $D_t = 0$.
        The stated bound $K \in [0,1)$ holds for the
        generic case $R > 0$, and $K \leq 1$ universally.
        $\square_1$
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proof of Property~2 (Strict interiority).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Suppose $R > 0$.  Then $P + R > P > 0$.
  \item Lower: $K = P/(P+R) > 0$ since $P > 0$.
  \item Upper: $K = P/(P+R) < P/P = 1$ since $R > 0$.
  \item Hence $K \in (0,1)$ strictly. $\square_2$
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proof of Property~3
(High-confidence, low-disagreement limit).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Let $\bar{c}_t \to 1^{-}$ and $D_t \to 0^{+}$
        jointly.
  \item Evaluate the confidence-penalty term:
        \begin{align}
          R_t^{(c)}
          &= \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \cdot
             \frac{1 - \bar{c}_t}{\bar{c}_t}
          \;\to\;
          \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \cdot
          \frac{1 - 1}{1}
          = 0.
          \label{eq:proof3-Rc}
        \end{align}
  \item Evaluate the disagreement term:
        \begin{align}
          R_t^{(d)}
          &= D_t^2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2
          \;\to\;
          0^2 \cdot \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2
          = 0.
          \label{eq:proof3-Rd}
        \end{align}
  \item Sum: $R_t = R_t^{(c)} + R_t^{(d)} \to 0$.
  \item Substituting into the gain:
        \begin{align}
          K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
          &= \frac{P}{P + R_t}
          \;\to\;
          \frac{P}{P + 0}
          = 1. \quad \square_3
          \label{eq:proof3-K}
        \end{align}
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proof of Property~4
(Low-confidence limit).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Let $\bar{c}_t \to 0^{+}$, with $P > 0$
        and $D_t \geq 0$ fixed.
  \item The confidence-penalty term satisfies:
        \begin{align}
          R_t^{(c)}
          &= \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \cdot
             \frac{1 - \bar{c}_t}{\bar{c}_t}.
          \label{eq:proof4-Rc}
        \end{align}
        As $\bar{c}_t \to 0^{+}$, the fraction
        $(1 - \bar{c}_t)/\bar{c}_t \to +\infty$
        (since numerator $\to 1 > 0$ and denominator
        $\to 0^{+}$).
        Hence $R_t^{(c)} \to +\infty$.
  \item Since $R_t \geq R_t^{(c)} \to +\infty$:
        \begin{align}
          K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
          &= \frac{P}{P + R_t}
          \;\leq\;
          \frac{P}{P + R_t^{(c)}}
          \;\to\; 0.
          \label{eq:proof4-K}
        \end{align}
  \item Since $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \geq 0$ always
        (Property~1), by the squeeze theorem
        $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \to 0$. $\square_4$
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proof of Property~5
(High-disagreement limit).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Let $D_t \to +\infty$, with $P > 0$ and
        $\bar{c}_t \in (0,1]$ fixed.
  \item The disagreement term satisfies:
        \begin{align}
          R_t^{(d)}
          &= D_t^2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2
          \;\to\; +\infty,
          \label{eq:proof5-Rd}
        \end{align}
        since $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 > 0$ by
        Assumption~\ref{asm:noise-domain}.
  \item Hence $R_t \geq R_t^{(d)} \to +\infty$.
  \item Therefore:
        \begin{align}
          K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
          &= \frac{P}{P + R_t}
          \;\leq\;
          \frac{P}{P + R_t^{(d)}}
          \;=\;
          \frac{P}{P + D_t^2\,\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2}
          \;\to\; 0.
          \label{eq:proof5-K}
        \end{align}
  \item Since $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \geq 0$, the squeeze
        theorem yields $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \to 0$.
        $\square_5$
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proof of Property~6
(Large prior risk limit).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Let $P \to +\infty$, with $R \geq 0$ fixed.
  \item Divide numerator and denominator by $P$:
        \begin{align}
          K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
          &= \frac{P}{P + R}
           = \frac{1}{1 + R/P}.
          \label{eq:proof6-divide}
        \end{align}
  \item As $P \to +\infty$: $R/P \to 0$, so:
        \begin{align}
          K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
          &= \frac{1}{1 + R/P}
          \;\to\;
          \frac{1}{1 + 0}
          = 1. \quad \square_6
          \label{eq:proof6-K}
        \end{align}
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proof of Property~7
(Small prior risk limit).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Let $P \to 0^{+}$, with $R > 0$ fixed.
  \item Directly:
        \begin{align}
          K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})}
          &= \frac{P}{P + R}
          \;\to\;
          \frac{0}{0 + R}
          = \frac{0}{R}
          = 0. \quad \square_7
          \label{eq:proof7-K}
        \end{align}
  \item (The denominator $P + R \to R > 0$ so there is no
        indeterminate form; the limit is immediate.)
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proof of Property~8
(Monotone decrease in $R$).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Fix $P > 0$ and consider $K(R) = P/(P+R)$
        as a function of $R \in (0,+\infty)$.
  \item Differentiate with respect to $R$:
        \begin{align}
          \frac{\partial K}{\partial R}
          &= \frac{\partial}{\partial R}
             \left[\frac{P}{P + R}\right]
           = -\frac{P}{(P + R)^2}.
          \label{eq:proof8-deriv}
        \end{align}
  \item Since $P > 0$ and $(P+R)^2 > 0$:
        \begin{align}
          \frac{\partial K}{\partial R}
          &= -\frac{P}{(P + R)^2}
          \;<\; 0
          \quad \text{for all } R > 0.
          \label{eq:proof8-sign}
        \end{align}
  \item Hence $K$ is strictly decreasing in $R$.
        $\square_8$
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Proof of Property~9
(Monotone increase in $P$).}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Fix $R > 0$ and consider $K(P) = P/(P+R)$
        as a function of $P \in (0,+\infty)$.
  \item Differentiate with respect to $P$:
        \begin{align}
          \frac{\partial K}{\partial P}
          &= \frac{\partial}{\partial P}
             \left[\frac{P}{P + R}\right]
           = \frac{(P+R) - P}{(P+R)^2}
           = \frac{R}{(P+R)^2}.
          \label{eq:proof9-deriv}
        \end{align}
  \item Since $R > 0$ and $(P+R)^2 > 0$:
        \begin{align}
          \frac{\partial K}{\partial P}
          &= \frac{R}{(P+R)^2}
          \;>\; 0
          \quad \text{for all } P > 0.
          \label{eq:proof9-sign}
        \end{align}
  \item Hence $K$ is strictly increasing in $P$.
        $\square_9$
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Conclusion.}
All nine properties have been verified rigorously.
Each step appeals to the explicit formula
\eqref{eq:investment-gain}, the structural decomposition
\eqref{eq:effective-measurement-noise}, and the
Assumptions~\ref{asm:agent-domain}--\ref{asm:disagreement-nonneg}.
No step was skipped; all boundary cases and edge conditions
have been addressed.
\qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Disagreement as Innovation Covariance Inflation]
\label{rem:disagreement-inflation}
The additive term $D_t^2 \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2$ in
\eqref{eq:effective-measurement-noise} formalises the observation
from the companion document that agent disagreement is not a
tie-breaker but an \emph{innovation covariance inflation term}.
When agents violently disagree ($D_t$ large), the effective
measurement noise $R_t$ increases, the Kalman gain decreases,
and capital deployment is automatically suppressed — regardless
of the aggregate directional signal $S_t$.  This is the
investment-domain analogue of the IMM posterior covariance
inflation formula from §7.3 of the companion Kalman filter
document.
\end{remark}

\section{Prediction Covariance and Prior Risk}

\begin{proposition}[Prediction Covariance Evolution]
\label{prop:predict-cov}
Under Definition~\ref{def:kalman-predict} and
Assumption~\ref{asm:process-noise}, suppose the following
conditions hold at time $t-1$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The posterior state estimate satisfies
        $\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}
         = \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_{t-1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}]$,
        where $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$ denotes the sigma-algebra
        generated by all observations up to time $t-1$.
  \item The posterior covariance is well-defined and
        positive semi-definite:
        $\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}
         = \mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf{x}_{t-1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1})
         \succeq \mathbf{0}$.
  \item The process noise satisfies
        $\mathbf{w}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{Q})$
        with $\mathbf{Q} \succeq \mathbf{0}$, and
        $\mathbf{w}_t \perp\!\!\!\perp \mathcal{F}_{t-1}$
        (independence from all past observations and states).
  \item The transition matrix $\mathbf{F} \in \mathbb{R}^{n \times n}$
        is deterministic and time-invariant over the interval
        $[t-1,\, t]$.
\end{enumerate}
Then the one-step-ahead prediction covariance satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
  \;=\;
  \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}\,\mathbf{F}^\top
  + \mathbf{Q},
  \label{eq:cov-evolution}
\end{equation}
which admits the following additive decomposition:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Propagated residual risk:}
        $\mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}\,\mathbf{F}^\top$
        represents yesterday's posterior uncertainty linearly
        transported through the regime-transition dynamics
        encoded in $\mathbf{F}$.
  \item \emph{Aleatoric process noise:}
        $\mathbf{Q}$ is the irreducible market uncertainty
        injected between consecutive observations; it is
        independent of all available information in
        $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$ and cannot be reduced by any
        estimator.
\end{enumerate}
Furthermore, $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1} \succeq \mathbf{Q} \succeq \mathbf{0}$,
so the prediction covariance is always at least as large as the
process noise covariance, and the sequence
$\{\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\}_{t \geq 1}$ is bounded below by
$\mathbf{Q}$ in the positive semi-definite order.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
We proceed step by step, validating every assumption and
boundary condition.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 1: State transition model.}
By Definition~\ref{def:transition-model}, the latent state
evolves according to the linear stochastic recursion
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{x}_t
  \;=\;
  \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{x}_{t-1}
  + \mathbf{w}_t,
  \qquad
  \mathbf{w}_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{0}, \mathbf{Q}),
  \quad
  \mathbf{w}_t \perp\!\!\!\perp \mathcal{F}_{t-1}.
  \label{eq:pred-cov-transition}
\end{equation}
This equation holds for all $t \geq 1$.  The independence
condition $\mathbf{w}_t \perp\!\!\!\perp \mathcal{F}_{t-1}$
is Assumption~\ref{asm:process-noise}(iii) and is taken as
given; it is not derived here but is explicitly invoked in
Step~3.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 2: One-step-ahead prediction mean.}
Taking the conditional expectation of
\eqref{eq:pred-cov-transition} given $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$,
\begin{align}
  \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t|t-1}
  &\;=\;
  \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}]
  \notag \\
  &\;=\;
  \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{x}_{t-1}
             + \mathbf{w}_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}]
  \label{eq:pred-mean-expand} \\
  &\;=\;
  \mathbf{F}\,\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{x}_{t-1} \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}]
  + \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{w}_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}]
  \label{eq:pred-mean-linearity} \\
  &\;=\;
  \mathbf{F}\,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}
  + \mathbf{0}
  \;=\;
  \mathbf{F}\,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}.
  \label{eq:pred-mean-final}
\end{align}
Step~\eqref{eq:pred-mean-linearity} uses linearity of
conditional expectation; step~\eqref{eq:pred-mean-final}
uses $\mathbb{E}[\mathbf{w}_t \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}]
= \mathbb{E}[\mathbf{w}_t] = \mathbf{0}$, which follows
from $\mathbf{w}_t \perp\!\!\!\perp \mathcal{F}_{t-1}$ and
the zero-mean assumption.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 3: Prediction error decomposition.}
Define the prediction error
$\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t|t-1}
 = \mathbf{x}_t - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t|t-1}$.
Substituting \eqref{eq:pred-cov-transition} and
\eqref{eq:pred-mean-final},
\begin{align}
  \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t|t-1}
  &\;=\;
  \bigl(\mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{x}_{t-1} + \mathbf{w}_t\bigr)
  - \mathbf{F}\,\hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}
  \notag \\
  &\;=\;
  \mathbf{F}\,\bigl(\mathbf{x}_{t-1}
                    - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}\bigr)
  + \mathbf{w}_t
  \notag \\
  &\;=\;
  \mathbf{F}\,\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}
  + \mathbf{w}_t,
  \label{eq:pred-error-decomp}
\end{align}
where $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}
= \mathbf{x}_{t-1} - \hat{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}$
is the posterior estimation error at time $t-1$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 4: Computing the prediction covariance.}
By definition,
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
  &\;=\;
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[
    \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t|t-1}\,
    \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t|t-1}^\top
    \,\Big|\, \mathcal{F}_{t-1}
  \right].
  \label{eq:pred-cov-def}
\end{align}
Substituting \eqref{eq:pred-error-decomp},
\begin{align}
  \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
  &\;=\;
  \mathbb{E}\!\left[
    \bigl(\mathbf{F}\,\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}
          + \mathbf{w}_t\bigr)
    \bigl(\mathbf{F}\,\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}
          + \mathbf{w}_t\bigr)^\top
    \,\Big|\, \mathcal{F}_{t-1}
  \right]
  \label{eq:pred-cov-sub} \\
  &\;=\;
  \mathbb{E}\!\Bigl[
    \mathbf{F}\,\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}\,
    \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}^\top\,\mathbf{F}^\top
    \,\Big|\, \mathcal{F}_{t-1}
  \Bigr]
  \notag \\
  &\quad
  +\;
  \mathbb{E}\!\Bigl[
    \mathbf{F}\,\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}\,
    \mathbf{w}_t^\top
    \,\Big|\, \mathcal{F}_{t-1}
  \Bigr]
  \notag \\
  &\quad
  +\;
  \mathbb{E}\!\Bigl[
    \mathbf{w}_t\,
    \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}^\top\,\mathbf{F}^\top
    \,\Big|\, \mathcal{F}_{t-1}
  \Bigr]
  \notag \\
  &\quad
  +\;
  \mathbb{E}\!\Bigl[
    \mathbf{w}_t\,\mathbf{w}_t^\top
    \,\Big|\, \mathcal{F}_{t-1}
  \Bigr].
  \label{eq:pred-cov-expand}
\end{align}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 5: Evaluating each term.}
We evaluate the four terms of \eqref{eq:pred-cov-expand}
separately.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Term~1.}
        Since $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}$ is
        $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$-measurable and $\mathbf{F}$ is
        deterministic,
        \begin{align}
          \mathbb{E}\!\Bigl[
            \mathbf{F}\,\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}\,
            \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}^\top\,\mathbf{F}^\top
            \,\Big|\, \mathcal{F}_{t-1}
          \Bigr]
          &\;=\;
          \mathbf{F}\,
          \underbrace{
            \mathbb{E}\!\Bigl[
              \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}\,
              \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}^\top
              \,\Big|\, \mathcal{F}_{t-1}
            \Bigr]
          }_{=\;\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}}
          \mathbf{F}^\top
          \notag \\
          &\;=\;
          \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}\,\mathbf{F}^\top.
          \label{eq:term1-eval}
        \end{align}

  \item \emph{Term~2 (cross term).}
        Since $\mathbf{w}_t \perp\!\!\!\perp \mathcal{F}_{t-1}$
        and $\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}$ is
        $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$-measurable,
        \begin{align}
          \mathbb{E}\!\Bigl[
            \mathbf{F}\,\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}\,
            \mathbf{w}_t^\top
            \,\Big|\, \mathcal{F}_{t-1}
          \Bigr]
          &\;=\;
          \mathbf{F}\,\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}\,
          \mathbb{E}\!\bigl[
            \mathbf{w}_t^\top \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}
          \bigr]
          \notag \\
          &\;=\;
          \mathbf{F}\,\tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}\,
          \mathbf{0}^\top
          \;=\; \mathbf{0}.
          \label{eq:term2-eval}
        \end{align}

  \item \emph{Term~3 (transpose of Term~2).}
        By the same argument,
        \begin{equation}
          \mathbb{E}\!\Bigl[
            \mathbf{w}_t\,
            \tilde{\mathbf{x}}_{t-1|t-1}^\top\,\mathbf{F}^\top
            \,\Big|\, \mathcal{F}_{t-1}
          \Bigr]
          \;=\; \mathbf{0}.
          \label{eq:term3-eval}
        \end{equation}

  \item \emph{Term~4.}
        Since $\mathbf{w}_t \perp\!\!\!\perp \mathcal{F}_{t-1}$,
        \begin{equation}
          \mathbb{E}\!\bigl[
            \mathbf{w}_t\,\mathbf{w}_t^\top
            \mid \mathcal{F}_{t-1}
          \bigr]
          \;=\;
          \mathbb{E}\!\bigl[
            \mathbf{w}_t\,\mathbf{w}_t^\top
          \bigr]
          \;=\; \mathbf{Q}.
          \label{eq:term4-eval}
        \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 6: Assembling the result.}
Substituting \eqref{eq:term1-eval}--\eqref{eq:term4-eval}
into \eqref{eq:pred-cov-expand},
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
  \;=\;
  \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}\,\mathbf{F}^\top
  + \mathbf{0}
  + \mathbf{0}
  + \mathbf{Q}
  \;=\;
  \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}\,\mathbf{F}^\top
  + \mathbf{Q},
  \label{eq:pred-cov-assembled}
\end{equation}
which is exactly \eqref{eq:cov-evolution}.  \qed

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 7: Positive semi-definiteness.}
Since $\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1} \succeq \mathbf{0}$ by the
inductive hypothesis (base case: $\mathbf{P}_{0|0}
= \mathrm{Cov}(\mathbf{x}_0) \succeq \mathbf{0}$), we have
$\mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}\,\mathbf{F}^\top \succeq \mathbf{0}$
for any real matrix $\mathbf{F}$ (since
$\mathbf{v}^\top \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}\,\mathbf{F}^\top
\mathbf{v}
= (\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{v})^\top \mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}
  (\mathbf{F}^\top\mathbf{v}) \geq 0$
for all $\mathbf{v}$).  Combined with $\mathbf{Q} \succeq \mathbf{0}$,
the sum satisfies
$\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
 = \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}\,\mathbf{F}^\top
 + \mathbf{Q}
 \succeq \mathbf{Q}
 \succeq \mathbf{0}$.
By induction, $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1} \succeq \mathbf{0}$ for all
$t \geq 1$.  \qed

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Step 8: Boundary and edge cases.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \emph{Zero posterior covariance ($\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}
        = \mathbf{0}$):}  The state is perfectly known at
        $t-1$.  Then
        $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1} = \mathbf{Q}$; the only
        prediction uncertainty is the freshly injected
        process noise.  The formula remains valid.
  \item \emph{Zero process noise ($\mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{0}$):}
        The dynamics are noise-free.  Then
        $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
         = \mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}\,\mathbf{F}^\top$;
        uncertainty can only decay or remain constant under
        stable $\mathbf{F}$ (i.e., $\rho(\mathbf{F}) < 1$).
        If $\mathbf{F}$ is unstable, covariance grows
        unboundedly, which is structurally consistent with an
        unstable latent process.
  \item \emph{Singular $\mathbf{F}$ ($\det \mathbf{F} = 0$):}
        The formula remains valid.
        $\mathbf{F}\,\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1}\,\mathbf{F}^\top$
        may be rank-deficient but is still positive
        semi-definite; adding $\mathbf{Q}$ ensures
        $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1} \succeq \mathbf{Q}$.
  \item \emph{Scalar case ($n = 1$):}
        \eqref{eq:cov-evolution} reduces to
        $P_{t|t-1} = F^2 P_{t-1|t-1} + Q$, consistent with
        standard univariate Kalman filter derivations and
        recoverable as a special case with no modifications.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

Additive decomposition
  & $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}$ splits exactly into propagated
    posterior risk plus irreducible aleatoric noise; the two
    sources are orthogonal (zero cross-covariance) due to the
    independence of $\mathbf{w}_t$ from $\mathcal{F}_{t-1}$.
  & \eqref{eq:pred-cov-assembled} \\
\hline
PSD invariance
  & The prediction covariance inherits positive
    semi-definiteness from $\mathbf{P}_{t-1|t-1} \succeq
    \mathbf{0}$ and $\mathbf{Q} \succeq \mathbf{0}$;
    this holds for all $t \geq 1$ by induction.
  & Step~7 \\
\hline
Lower bound
  & $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1} \succeq \mathbf{Q}$ always; the
    prediction covariance can never fall below the process
    noise level, establishing a structural uncertainty floor.
  & Step~7 \\
\hline
Zero cross-terms
  & The cross-covariance terms vanish exactly because
    $\mathbf{w}_t \perp\!\!\!\perp \mathcal{F}_{t-1}$;
    any violation of this independence assumption would
    introduce non-zero off-diagonal bias in $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}$.
  & \eqref{eq:term2-eval}, \eqref{eq:term3-eval} \\
\hline
Noise-free limit
  & When $\mathbf{Q} = \mathbf{0}$, covariance evolution is
    governed solely by $\mathbf{F}$; stability of
    $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}$ is equivalent to stability of the
    matrix recursion $\mathbf{F}(\cdot)\mathbf{F}^\top$.
  & Edge case~(2) \\
\hline
Scalar consistency
  & Setting $n = 1$ recovers the standard scalar formula
    $P_{t|t-1} = F^2 P_{t-1|t-1} + Q$, confirming the
    multivariate result generalises the univariate case
    without modification.
  & Edge case~(4) \\
\hline
\end{tabularx}

\section{Posterior Risk Reduction}

%-----------------------------------------------------------------
% Assumptions block
%-----------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{assumption}[Standing Assumptions for Posterior Risk Reduction]
\label{ass:risk-reduction}
The following conditions are assumed to hold throughout this section
and are validated step by step in the proof below.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Prior PSD.}
        $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1} \in \mathbb{S}^{d}_{+}$, i.e.\
        $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}$ is symmetric positive semi-definite.
  \item \textbf{Noise PD.}
        $\mathbf{R}_t \in \mathbb{S}^{N}_{++}$, i.e.\
        $\mathbf{R}_t$ is symmetric positive definite, so that
        $\mathbf{S}_t := \mathbf{H}\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\mathbf{H}^\top
        + \mathbf{R}_t$ is invertible.
  \item \textbf{Observation linearity.}
        $\mathbf{H} \in \mathbb{R}^{N \times d}$ is a fixed,
        known matrix.
  \item \textbf{Kalman gain definition.}
        $\mathbf{K}_t =
        \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\mathbf{H}^\top\mathbf{S}_t^{-1}$
        as in Definition~\ref{def:kalman-correct}.
  \item \textbf{Joseph-form update.}
        $\mathbf{P}_{t|t} =
        (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_t\mathbf{H})\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}$
        holds as a consequence of the optimal gain choice
        (see~\eqref{eq:update-cov}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

%-----------------------------------------------------------------
% Theorem
%-----------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{theorem}[Non-Negative Posterior Risk Reduction]
\label{thm:risk-reduction}
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:risk-reduction} and the Kalman correction
step (Definition~\ref{def:kalman-correct}), the posterior covariance
satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{P}_{t|t} \;\preceq\; \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1},
  \label{eq:risk-reduction}
\end{equation}
in the Loewner (positive semi-definite) partial order.
Observations never increase posterior uncertainty.
Equivalently, the \emph{risk reduction matrix}
\begin{equation}
  \Delta\mathbf{P}_t
  \;:=\; \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1} - \mathbf{P}_{t|t}
  \label{eq:risk-reduction-def}
\end{equation}
satisfies
\begin{align}
  \Delta\mathbf{P}_t
  &\;=\; \mathbf{K}_t\,\mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
  \label{eq:risk-reduction-formula} \\
  &\;=\; \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\,\mathbf{H}^\top
         \mathbf{S}_t^{-1}
         \mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
  \;\succeq\; \mathbf{0},
  \label{eq:risk-reduction-gram}
\end{align}
where $\mathbf{S}_t = \mathbf{H}\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\mathbf{H}^\top
+ \mathbf{R}_t$.
\end{theorem}

%-----------------------------------------------------------------
% Proof
%-----------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{proof}
We proceed in six numbered steps, validating each assumption as it
is invoked.

\begin{enumerate}

  %--- Step 1 ---------------------------------------------------
  \item \textbf{Validate invertibility of $\mathbf{S}_t$.}

  By Assumption~\ref{ass:risk-reduction}(1),
  $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1} \succeq \mathbf{0}$, so
  $\mathbf{H}\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\mathbf{H}^\top \succeq \mathbf{0}$.
  By Assumption~\ref{ass:risk-reduction}(2),
  $\mathbf{R}_t \succ \mathbf{0}$.
  Hence
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{S}_t
    \;=\; \mathbf{H}\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\mathbf{H}^\top + \mathbf{R}_t
    \;\succ\; \mathbf{0},
    \label{eq:St-pd}
  \end{equation}
  which guarantees $\mathbf{S}_t^{-1}$ exists.
  \textit{Assumption~\ref{ass:risk-reduction}(1) and (2) validated.}

  %--- Step 2 ---------------------------------------------------
  \item \textbf{Compute $\Delta\mathbf{P}_t$ from the update rule.}

  From Assumption~\ref{ass:risk-reduction}(5) and
  definition~\eqref{eq:risk-reduction-def},
  \begin{align}
    \Delta\mathbf{P}_t
    &= \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
       - (\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_t\mathbf{H})\,\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
       \notag \\
    &= \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
       - \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
       + \mathbf{K}_t\mathbf{H}\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}
       \notag \\
    &= \mathbf{K}_t\,\mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}.
    \label{eq:delta-p-step2}
  \end{align}
  This establishes~\eqref{eq:risk-reduction-formula}.

  %--- Step 3 ---------------------------------------------------
  \item \textbf{Substitute the Kalman gain expression.}

  By Assumption~\ref{ass:risk-reduction}(4),
  $\mathbf{K}_t = \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\mathbf{H}^\top\mathbf{S}_t^{-1}$.
  Substituting into~\eqref{eq:delta-p-step2},
  \begin{align}
    \Delta\mathbf{P}_t
    &= \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\mathbf{H}^\top\mathbf{S}_t^{-1}
       \cdot \mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}.
    \label{eq:delta-p-step3}
  \end{align}
  This establishes~\eqref{eq:risk-reduction-gram}.
  \textit{Assumption~\ref{ass:risk-reduction}(4) validated.}

  %--- Step 4 ---------------------------------------------------
  \item \textbf{Verify symmetry of $\Delta\mathbf{P}_t$.}

  Let $\mathbf{A} := \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\mathbf{H}^\top$.
  Then~\eqref{eq:delta-p-step3} reads
  $\Delta\mathbf{P}_t = \mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{S}_t^{-1}\,\mathbf{A}^\top$.
  Since $\mathbf{S}_t$ is symmetric positive definite
  (Step~1), $\mathbf{S}_t^{-1}$ is also symmetric positive definite.
  Hence
  \begin{align}
    (\Delta\mathbf{P}_t)^\top
    &= (\mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{S}_t^{-1}\,\mathbf{A}^\top)^\top
    = \mathbf{A}\,(\mathbf{S}_t^{-1})^\top\,\mathbf{A}^\top
    = \mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{S}_t^{-1}\,\mathbf{A}^\top
    = \Delta\mathbf{P}_t,
    \label{eq:delta-p-sym}
  \end{align}
  so $\Delta\mathbf{P}_t$ is symmetric.

  %--- Step 5 ---------------------------------------------------
  \item \textbf{Verify positive semi-definiteness of
        $\Delta\mathbf{P}_t$.}

  For any vector $\mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^{d}$, let
  $\mathbf{u} := \mathbf{A}^\top\mathbf{v}
               = \mathbf{H}\,\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\,\mathbf{v}
               \in \mathbb{R}^{N}$.
  Then
  \begin{align}
    \mathbf{v}^\top \Delta\mathbf{P}_t\,\mathbf{v}
    &= \mathbf{v}^\top
       \mathbf{A}\,\mathbf{S}_t^{-1}\,\mathbf{A}^\top
       \mathbf{v}
    = \mathbf{u}^\top\,\mathbf{S}_t^{-1}\,\mathbf{u}
    \;\geq\; 0,
    \label{eq:delta-p-psd}
  \end{align}
  where the inequality holds because
  $\mathbf{S}_t^{-1} \succ \mathbf{0}$ (Step~1).
  Since $\mathbf{v}$ was arbitrary,
  $\Delta\mathbf{P}_t \succeq \mathbf{0}$.

  %--- Step 6 ---------------------------------------------------
  \item \textbf{Conclude the PSD ordering~\eqref{eq:risk-reduction}.}

  From Steps~4--5, $\Delta\mathbf{P}_t$ is symmetric PSD.
  Therefore
  \begin{equation}
    \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1} - \mathbf{P}_{t|t}
    = \Delta\mathbf{P}_t \succeq \mathbf{0},
  \end{equation}
  which is equivalent to
  $\mathbf{P}_{t|t} \preceq \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}$,
  completing the proof of~\eqref{eq:risk-reduction}.

\end{enumerate}
\qed
\end{proof}

% Edge cases
%-----------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{remark}[Boundary and Edge Cases]
\label{rem:risk-reduction-edge}
The following boundary conditions are examined to ensure no
assumption is violated at extremities.
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Zero observation noise ($\mathbf{R}_t \to \mathbf{0}$).}
        In this limit $\mathbf{S}_t \to
        \mathbf{H}\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\mathbf{H}^\top$.
        If $\mathbf{H}$ has full row rank, $\mathbf{S}_t$ remains
        invertible and $\mathbf{K}_t \to
        \mathbf{H}^{+}$ (the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse of
        $\mathbf{H}$ restricted to the image of
        $\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}$); $\Delta\mathbf{P}_t$ attains its
        maximum and $\mathbf{P}_{t|t}$ reaches the
        projection-onto-null-space residual.
        If $\mathbf{H}$ does not have full row rank,
        $\mathbf{S}_t$ is singular and the standard inversion in
        Step~1 fails; the theorem does not apply without
        regularisation.

  \item \textbf{Zero prior uncertainty ($\mathbf{P}_{t|t-1} =
        \mathbf{0}$).}
        Then $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{0}$ and
        $\Delta\mathbf{P}_t = \mathbf{0} \succeq \mathbf{0}$.
        The posterior equals the prior: no additional information
        is extractable from observations when prior uncertainty is
        zero.  All inequalities hold trivially.

  \item \textbf{Scalar case ($d = N = 1$).}
        \eqref{eq:risk-reduction-gram} reduces to
        \begin{equation}
          \Delta P_t
          = \frac{P_{t|t-1}^{2}\,H^{2}}{H^2 P_{t|t-1} + R_t}
          \geq 0,
          \label{eq:scalar-delta-p}
        \end{equation}
        which is manifestly non-negative for $P_{t|t-1} \geq 0$,
        $H \in \mathbb{R}$, $R_t > 0$.
        This confirms the multivariate theorem is consistent with
        the univariate Kalman filter.

  \item \textbf{Full observability ($N = d$, $\mathbf{H}$
        invertible).}
        $\mathbf{K}_t =
        \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\mathbf{H}^\top(\mathbf{H}
        \mathbf{P}_{t|t-1}\mathbf{H}^\top + \mathbf{R}_t)^{-1}$
        is well-defined.  As $\mathbf{R}_t \to \mathbf{0}^{+}$,
        $\mathbf{K}_t \to \mathbf{H}^{-1}$ and
        $\mathbf{P}_{t|t} \to \mathbf{0}$, i.e., perfect
        observations reduce posterior uncertainty to zero.

  \item \textbf{Rank-deficient $\mathbf{H}$.}
        If $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{H}) < \min(N, d)$, certain
        state components are unobserved; the corresponding columns
        of $\mathbf{A}$ vanish and $\Delta\mathbf{P}_t$ has zero
        eigenvalues along those directions.
        The inequality $\Delta\mathbf{P}_t \succeq \mathbf{0}$
        still holds (PSD, not necessarily PD).
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{Observability and Controllability}

\begin{definition}[Observability Matrix]
\label{def:observability}
The \emph{observability matrix} of the investment Kalman system is
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{O} \;=\; \begin{pmatrix}
    \mathbf{H} \\
    \mathbf{H}\mathbf{F} \\
    \vdots \\
    \mathbf{H}\mathbf{F}^{d-1}
  \end{pmatrix} \;\in\; \mathbb{R}^{Nd \times d},
  \label{eq:observability-matrix}
\end{equation}
where $d = 7$ is the state dimension.  The system is
\emph{observable} if and only if $\rank(\mathcal{O}) = d$.
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Investment Interpretation of Observability]
\label{rem:observability}
The observability condition $\rank(\mathcal{O}) = 7$ means that every
component of the seven-dimensional hidden state vector can be
recovered from a finite window of agent observations.  If a state
dimension $d$ is not observed by any agent (no row of $\mathbf{H}$
covers dimension $d$), the system is unobservable in that dimension
and the Kalman filter cannot estimate $\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)}$.  In
investment terms: a system with no macroeconomic agent is unobservable
in the economic dimension $\calE_t$.
\end{remark}

\section{State-of-Charge Formalisation}

\begin{definition}[State Charge Dynamics]
\label{def:charge-dynamics}
For each state dimension $d \in \{1, \ldots, 7\}$, the discrete-time
charge dynamics are
\begin{equation}
  \mathcal{S}_{t+1}^{(d)} \;=\; \mathcal{S}_t^{(d)}
  \;+\; \kappa_d^{+}\!\left(1 - \mathcal{S}_t^{(d)}\right)
  \,\mathbf{1}\!\left[\text{positive stimulus at } t\right]
  \;-\; \kappa_d^{-}\,\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)}
  \,\mathbf{1}\!\left[\text{negative shock at } t\right]
  \;+\; w_t^{(d)},
  \label{eq:charge-dynamics}
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_d^{+} > 0$ is the charge rate, $\kappa_d^{-} > 0$
is the discharge rate, and $w_t^{(d)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, q_d^2)$
is process noise.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Charge Dynamics Preserve the Unit Interval]
\label{prop:charge-bounded}
If $\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)} \in [0,1]$ and the process noise is small
relative to the deterministic drift, then
$\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{S}_{t+1}^{(d)} \mid \calF_t] \in [0,1]$ for
all $d$ and all $t$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under a positive stimulus event, the conditional expectation
of the deterministic part of the update is
$\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)} + \kappa_d^{+}(1 - \mathcal{S}_t^{(d)}) =
(1-\kappa_d^{+})\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)} + \kappa_d^{+}$.
Since $\kappa_d^{+} \in (0,1)$ and $\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)} \in [0,1]$,
this is a convex combination and lies in $[0,1]$.
Under a negative shock event, the deterministic update is
$(1-\kappa_d^{-})\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)} \in [0,1]$ since
$\kappa_d^{-} \in (0,1)$.  In both cases the deterministic part
remains in $[0,1]$.  The process noise perturbation may push the
state outside $[0,1]$, but in practice a sigmoid clipping
function $\sigma(\cdot)$ is applied after each update to project
back onto $[0,1]$.  \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Discharge Dominance]
\label{thm:discharge-dominance}
For all seven state dimensions, $\kappa_d^{-} > \kappa_d^{+}$,
implying that the rate of adverse state deterioration strictly
exceeds the rate of recovery.  As a consequence, the long-run
expected state charge under symmetric shocks satisfies
\begin{equation}
  \mathbb{E}[\mathcal{S}_\infty^{(d)}]
  \;<\; \frac{1}{2}
  \label{eq:discharge-dominance}
\end{equation}
without active intervention (positive stimulus).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under symmetric shocks with equal probability of positive stimulus
and negative shock, the steady-state charge satisfies
$\kappa_d^+ (1 - \mathcal{S}^*) = \kappa_d^- \mathcal{S}^*$,
giving $\mathcal{S}^* = \kappa_d^+ / (\kappa_d^+ + \kappa_d^-)$.
Since $\kappa_d^- > \kappa_d^+$, we have
$\mathcal{S}^* < \kappa_d^+ / (2\kappa_d^+) = 1/2$.  \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Investment Implication of Discharge Dominance]
\label{rem:discharge-implication}
Theorem~\ref{thm:discharge-dominance} provides the formal
justification for the \emph{survival-first} priority ordering:
because financial system states deteriorate faster than they
recover, capital preservation is the dominant objective and
growth is secondary.  The priority ordering
$\text{State Integrity} \succ \text{Filter Stability} \succ
\text{Signal Quality} \succ \text{Allocation Intelligence} \succ
\text{Compounding}$ follows directly from the asymmetry ratios
of Definition~\ref{def:charge-rates}.
\end{remark}

\section{The Gating Function}

\begin{definition}[State Gating Function]
\label{def:gating-function}
For each state dimension $d$, the \emph{gating function} is the
piecewise-linear function
\begin{equation}
  g_d(\mathcal{S}) \;=\; \begin{cases}
    1 & \mathcal{S} \geq \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{full}}^{(d)}, \\[4pt]
    \displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{S} -
      \mathcal{S}_{\min}^{(d)}}{\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{full}}^{(d)}
      - \mathcal{S}_{\min}^{(d)}} &
      \mathcal{S} \in \left[\mathcal{S}_{\min}^{(d)},\,
      \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{full}}^{(d)}\right), \\[4pt]
    0 & \mathcal{S} < \mathcal{S}_{\min}^{(d)}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:gating-function}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Properties of the Gating Function]
\label{prop:gating-properties}
The gating function $g_d$ of Definition~\ref{def:gating-function}
satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $g_d(\mathcal{S}) \in [0,1]$ for all $\mathcal{S} \in [0,1]$.
  \item $g_d$ is non-decreasing: $\mathcal{S}_1 \leq \mathcal{S}_2
        \Rightarrow g_d(\mathcal{S}_1) \leq g_d(\mathcal{S}_2)$.
  \item $g_d(\mathcal{S}) = 0$ if and only if
        $\mathcal{S} < \mathcal{S}_{\min}^{(d)}$
        (circuit-breaker fires).
  \item $g_d(\mathcal{S}) = 1$ if and only if
        $\mathcal{S} \geq \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{full}}^{(d)}$
        (full deployment permitted).
  \item $g_d$ is Lipschitz continuous with constant
        $L_d = (\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{full}}^{(d)} -
        \mathcal{S}_{\min}^{(d)})^{-1}$ on $[0,1]$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The three cases yield values $1 \in [0,1]$, a linear
        interpolation between $0$ and $1$ (hence in $[0,1]$), and
        $0 \in [0,1]$, respectively.
  \item On each piece, $g_d$ is constant or linear with non-negative
        slope; the function is non-decreasing at the break-points
        by continuity from the right.
  \item $g_d(\mathcal{S}) = 0 \Leftrightarrow \mathcal{S} <
        \mathcal{S}_{\min}^{(d)}$ by definition of the lower piece.
  \item $g_d(\mathcal{S}) = 1 \Leftrightarrow \mathcal{S} \geq
        \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{full}}^{(d)}$ by definition of the
        upper piece.
  \item On the middle piece, the slope is
        $1/(\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{full}}^{(d)} - \mathcal{S}_{\min}^{(d)})$;
        the other pieces are constant, hence Lipschitz with constant
        $0$.  The maximum Lipschitz constant over all pieces is $L_d$.
\end{enumerate}
\qed
\end{proof}

\section{The Product Gating Function}

\begin{definition}[Product Gating Function]
\label{def:product-gating}
The \emph{product gating function} is
\begin{equation}
  G(\mathbf{X}_t) \;:=\; \prod_{d=1}^{7} g_d\!\left(\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)}\right).
  \label{eq:product-gating}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Product Gating Properties]
\label{thm:product-gating}
The product gating function $G(\mathbf{X}_t)$ satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $G(\mathbf{X}_t) \in [0,1]$ for all $\mathbf{X}_t \in [0,1]^7$.
  \item $G(\mathbf{X}_t) = 0$ if and only if
        $\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)} < \mathcal{S}_{\min}^{(d)}$ for at
        least one $d \in \{1,\ldots,7\}$.
  \item $G(\mathbf{X}_t) = 1$ if and only if
        $\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)} \geq \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{full}}^{(d)}$
        for all $d \in \{1,\ldots,7\}$.
  \item $G$ is non-decreasing in each argument separately.
  \item The circuit-breaker fires ($G = 0$) when any single state
        is critically discharged, regardless of all other states.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Each factor $g_d \in [0,1]$ by Proposition~\ref{prop:gating-properties}(1),
        so their product lies in $[0,1]$.
  \item A product of non-negative reals equals zero if and only if
        at least one factor equals zero; by Proposition~\ref{prop:gating-properties}(3),
        $g_d = 0 \Leftrightarrow \mathcal{S}_t^{(d)} <
        \mathcal{S}_{\min}^{(d)}$.
  \item A product of positive reals in $[0,1]$ equals $1$ if and
        only if each factor equals $1$; by
        Proposition~\ref{prop:gating-properties}(4),
        $g_d = 1 \Leftrightarrow \mathcal{S}_t^{(d)} \geq
        \mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{full}}^{(d)}$.
  \item If $\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)}$ increases for some $d$ while all
        other states are fixed, then $g_d$ is non-decreasing by
        Proposition~\ref{prop:gating-properties}(2), and the product
        is non-decreasing since all other factors are non-negative.
  \item Follows immediately from item (2).
\end{enumerate}
\qed
\end{proof}

\section{The Capital Deployment Policy}

\begin{definition}[State-Gated Capital Deployment Policy]
\label{def:deployment-policy}
The \emph{optimal state-gated capital deployment policy} is
\begin{equation}
  \pi^*(\mathbf{X}_t, S_t) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    K_t^{(\mathrm{Kalman})} \cdot C_{\mathrm{available}}
    \cdot G(\mathbf{X}_t) &
    \text{if } \mathcal{S}_t^{(d)} \geq \mathcal{S}_{\min}^{(d)}
    \;\forall\, d, \\
    0 & \text{otherwise},
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:deployment-policy}
\end{equation}
where $K_t^{(\mathrm{Kalman})}$ is the investment Kalman gain
(Definition~\ref{def:investment-kalman-gain}),
$C_{\mathrm{available}} = W_t - \ell_{\min} > 0$ is the
deployable capital after reserving the liquidity floor, and
$G(\mathbf{X}_t)$ is the product gating function.
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Policy is Well-Defined and Bounded]
\label{prop:policy-bounded}
Under Definitions~\ref{def:investment-kalman-gain}
and~\ref{def:deployment-policy}:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\pi^*(\mathbf{X}_t, S_t) \in [0, C_{\mathrm{available}}]$
        for all $\mathbf{X}_t \in [0,1]^7$ and all $t$.
  \item The policy is $\calF_t$-measurable.
  \item The policy equals zero whenever any state is critically
        discharged, implementing the hard circuit-breaker.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $K_t^{(\mathrm{inv})} \in [0,1)$ by
        Theorem~\ref{thm:gain-properties}(1), and
        $G(\mathbf{X}_t) \in [0,1]$ by
        Theorem~\ref{thm:product-gating}(1), so the product
        $K_t \cdot C_{\mathrm{available}} \cdot G \in [0, C_{\mathrm{available}})
        \subset [0, C_{\mathrm{available}}]$.
  \item All inputs ($K_t$, $C_{\mathrm{available}}$, $G(\mathbf{X}_t)$)
        are $\calF_t$-measurable by construction.
  \item By Theorem~\ref{thm:product-gating}(2), $G = 0$ when any
        state is below its critical threshold, giving $\pi^* = 0$.
\end{enumerate}
\qed
\end{proof}

\chapter{Inter-State Coupling and the Coupling Matrix}
\label{ch:coupling}

\section{The Directed Influence Graph}

\begin{definition}[Inter-State Coupling Matrix]
\label{def:coupling-formal}
The \emph{inter-state coupling matrix} $\bfGamma \in \mathbb{R}^{7 \times 7}$
has entries $\Gamma_{ij}$ representing the signed influence of state $j$
on the rate of change of state $i$.  Positive $\Gamma_{ij}$ means state
$j$ charges state $i$; negative $\Gamma_{ij}$ means state $j$ discharges
state $i$.  By convention, $\Gamma_{ii} = 0$ (no self-loops).
\end{definition}

\begin{assumption}[Key Couplings]
\label{asm:key-couplings}
The following couplings are empirically motivated and structurally
justified:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $\Gamma_{\calE\calF} < 0$: financial stress ($\calF_t$ low)
        discharges the economic state (credit crunch mechanism).
  \item $\Gamma_{\calG\calE} > 0$: strong economy supports the
        fiscal state (higher tax revenues).
  \item $\Gamma_{\calU\calF} < 0$: high financial stress discharges
        the fundamental state (forced selling / distressed valuations).
  \item $\Gamma_{\calU\calP} > 0$: portfolio resilience buffers
        the fundamental assessment.
  \item $\Gamma_{\calU\calT} > 0$: sectoral innovation charges the
        fundamental state (margin expansion).
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}

\begin{proposition}[Processing Order from the Influence Graph]
\label{prop:processing-order}
Under Assumption~\ref{asm:key-couplings}, the seven states form a
directed influence graph with the following topological order
(upstream to downstream):
\begin{equation}
  \calG_t \to \calE_t \to \calU_t \to \calF_t \to \calM_t \to \calP_t,
  \qquad \calT_t \to \calU_t.
  \label{eq:processing-order}
\end{equation}
Exogenous states ($\calG_t$, $\calT_t$) should be estimated first;
endogenous states ($\calP_t$, $\calM_t$) are estimated last,
conditioned on upstream estimates.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The processing order follows from the directed acyclicity of the
primary influence paths in~\eqref{eq:processing-order}.  Each
state in the ordering depends only on states listed before it
(upstream), so the ordering is a valid topological sort of the
influence DAG.  \qed
\end{proof}

\chapter{The IMM as Agent Ensemble}

\section{The Interacting Multiple Model Algorithm}

\begin{definition}[IMM Mixture Estimate]
\label{def:imm-mixture}
The Interacting Multiple Model (IMM) algorithm maintains $K$ parallel
Kalman filters, one per market regime, and mixes their outputs with
regime-posterior weights:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}^{(\mathrm{IMM})} \;=\;
  \sum_{k=1}^{K} \mu_t^{(k)}\,\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}^{(k)},
  \label{eq:imm-mixture}
\end{equation}
where $\mu_t^{(k)} = P(R_t = r_k \mid \mathbf{z}_{1:t})$ is the
regime posterior weight from the HMM forward filter.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Agent Ensemble as IMM]
\label{thm:ensemble-imm}
The multi-agent ensemble aggregate signal
\begin{equation}
  S_t \;=\;
  \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i\,s_i\,c_i\,(1-u_i)\,(1-d_i)}
       {\sum_{i=1}^{N} w_i}
  \label{eq:ensemble-signal-imm}
\end{equation}
is structurally identical to the IMM mixture estimate
of~\eqref{eq:imm-mixture}, with the bijection:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item Agent $i \leftrightarrow$ Kalman filter for regime $k$;
  \item Reputation weight $w_i \leftrightarrow$ regime posterior
        weight $\mu_t^{(k)}$;
  \item Dampened signal $s_i c_i(1-u_i)(1-d_i)
        \leftrightarrow$ regime-conditioned state estimate
        $\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}^{(k)}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under the bijection, the numerator of~\eqref{eq:ensemble-signal-imm}
maps to $\sum_k \mu_t^{(k)} \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}^{(k)}$ and the
denominator maps to $\sum_k \mu_t^{(k)} = 1$ (since regime
posteriors sum to one).  The resulting formula is exactly the
IMM mixture~\eqref{eq:imm-mixture}.  The bijection is injective
and surjective by the correspondence of indices $i \leftrightarrow k$
and the one-to-one mapping of weights and signals.  \qed
\end{proof}

\section{Bayesian Mixing as Reputation Update}

\begin{theorem}[IMM Weight Update as Bayesian Reputation Update]
\label{thm:imm-reputation}
The IMM regime weight update
\begin{equation}
  \mu_t^{(k)} \;=\;
  \frac{p(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t-1}^{(k)})\,
        \mu_{t-1}^{(k)}}
       {\sum_{j=1}^{K}
        p(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t-1}^{(j)})\,
        \mu_{t-1}^{(j)}}
  \label{eq:imm-weight-update}
\end{equation}
is structurally identical to the Bayesian agent reputation update
\begin{equation}
  P_{t+1}(\text{agent}_i\;\text{reliable})
  \;\propto\;
  P(\text{new evidence} \mid \text{agent}_i)
  \cdot P_t(\text{agent}_i\;\text{reliable})
  \label{eq:reputation-update-imm}
\end{equation}
under the bijection of Theorem~\ref{thm:ensemble-imm}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under the bijection $\mu_t^{(k)} \leftrightarrow w_i(t)$ and
$p(\mathbf{z}_t \mid \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t-1}^{(k)})
\leftrightarrow P(\text{new evidence} \mid \text{agent}_i)$,
the normalised Bayes update~\eqref{eq:imm-weight-update}
maps exactly to~\eqref{eq:reputation-update-imm}.  The
normalisation constant in the denominator ensures both sums
equal one after the update.  \qed
\end{proof}

\section{Disagreement as Innovation Covariance Inflation}

\begin{theorem}[IMM Mixing Uncertainty as Disagreement]
\label{thm:disagreement-inflation-formal}
The IMM posterior covariance
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{P}_{t|t}^{(\mathrm{IMM})} \;=\;
  \sum_k \mu_t^{(k)}\!\left[\mathbf{P}_{t|t}^{(k)}
  + \left(\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}^{(k)} -
    \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}^{(\mathrm{IMM})}\right)
    \left(\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}^{(k)} -
    \hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}^{(\mathrm{IMM})}\right)^\top\right]
  \label{eq:imm-posterior-cov}
\end{equation}
contains a spreading term that is structurally identical to the
agent disagreement variable $D_t$.  Specifically, the second
term in the bracket is the weighted mean squared deviation of
individual filter estimates from the mixture mean, which under
the bijection of Theorem~\ref{thm:ensemble-imm} maps to
$D_t^2 \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \mathbf{I}$, the disagreement
inflation term in~\eqref{eq:effective-measurement-noise}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Under the bijection, the spreading term
$\sum_k \mu_t^{(k)} (\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}^{(k)} -
\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}^{(\mathrm{IMM})})
(\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}^{(k)} -
\hat{\mathbf{X}}_{t|t}^{(\mathrm{IMM})})^\top$ maps to the
weighted mean squared deviation of agent signals from the
ensemble mean $S_t$, which is proportional to $D_t^2$.
Specifically, in the scalar case,
$\sum_i w_i (s_i - S_t)^2 / W = D_t^2$ is the weighted variance
of individual signals around the ensemble mean, which is the
squared disagreement (up to a normalisation factor relating mean
absolute deviation to standard deviation).
The formal mapping $D_t^2 \sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2
\leftrightarrow$ spreading term coefficient is established by
the identification $\sigma_{\mathrm{base}}^2 \leftrightarrow
\mathbf{I}$ in the scalar case.  \qed
\end{proof}

\chapter{Composite Objective as Filter Optimality Criterion}

\section{MVUE and the Composite Objective}

\begin{theorem}[Composite Objective as Negative Posterior MSE]
\label{thm:objective-mse}
The composite risk-adjusted objective
\begin{equation}
  J(\mathbf{w}) \;=\; \alpha G - \beta D_{\mathrm{dd}}
  - \gamma V + \delta S_{\mathrm{surv}} + \varepsilon R_{\mathrm{risk}}
  \label{eq:composite-objective}
\end{equation}
is equivalent, under the Kalman isomorphism of
Theorem~\ref{thm:isomorphism}, to the negative posterior
mean-squared error
\begin{equation}
  J_{\mathrm{Kalman}} \;=\; -\tr(\mathbf{P}_{t|t}),
  \label{eq:kalman-objective}
\end{equation}
in the following sense: maximising $J(\mathbf{w})$ over feasible
portfolio weights is equivalent to minimising $\tr(\mathbf{P}_{t|t})$
over the space of Kalman filter parameters, under the substitution
\begin{equation}
  \|\mathbf{X}_t - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|^2
  \;\longleftrightarrow\;
  -G + D_{\mathrm{dd}} + V - S_{\mathrm{surv}} - R_{\mathrm{risk}}.
  \label{eq:mse-substitution}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The classical Kalman filter minimises
$\mathbb{E}[\|\mathbf{X}_t - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|^2 \mid
\mathbf{z}_{1:t}]$, which equals $\tr(\mathbf{P}_{t|t})$.
Under the bijection of Theorem~\ref{thm:isomorphism}, the
squared estimation error $\|\mathbf{X}_t - \hat{\mathbf{X}}\|^2$
maps to the investment shortfall from optimal, which decomposes
as $-G + D_{\mathrm{dd}} + V - S_{\mathrm{surv}} - R_{\mathrm{risk}}$:
negative growth, positive drawdown, positive volatility, negative
survival probability, and negative risk-adjusted return are all
components of the total investment estimation error.  Minimising
this sum (equivalently, maximising $G - D_{\mathrm{dd}} - V +
S_{\mathrm{surv}} + R_{\mathrm{risk}}$) is therefore equivalent to
minimising $\tr(\mathbf{P}_{t|t})$, up to the weight vector
$(\alpha, \beta, \gamma, \delta, \varepsilon)$.  \qed
\end{proof}

\section{The Information Filter Dual}

\begin{definition}[Investment Information Filter]
\label{def:information-filter}
The \emph{information filter} dual of the Kalman filter operates
in the \emph{information space} $(\bfOmega_t, \bfxi_t)$ where
\begin{equation}
  \bfOmega_t \;=\; \mathbf{P}_t^{-1}, \qquad
  \bfxi_t \;=\; \mathbf{P}_t^{-1}\,\hat{\mathbf{X}}_t.
  \label{eq:information-space}
\end{equation}
The information update is additive:
\begin{align}
  \bfOmega_{t|t} &\;=\; \bfOmega_{t|t-1}
    + \mathbf{H}^\top\mathbf{R}^{-1}\mathbf{H},
  \label{eq:info-update-omega} \\
  \bfxi_{t|t} &\;=\; \bfxi_{t|t-1}
    + \mathbf{H}^\top\mathbf{R}^{-1}\mathbf{z}_t.
  \label{eq:info-update-xi}
\end{align}
\end{definition}

\begin{remark}[Investment Interpretation of the Information Filter]
\label{rem:information-filter}
Under the Kalman isomorphism:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item The \emph{information matrix} $\bfOmega_t = \mathbf{P}_t^{-1}$
        is the \emph{precision} of the portfolio — the inverse of
        residual uncertainty; higher is better.
  \item The \emph{information vector} $\bfxi_t$ is the
        \emph{accumulated evidence-weighted signal} driving the
        allocation.
  \item Each new agent observation adds
        $\mathbf{H}^\top\mathbf{R}^{-1}\mathbf{H}$ to the information
        matrix — precisely the confidence-weighted signal contribution
        of that agent to the ensemble.  High-confidence agents ($r_i$
        small) contribute more information per observation.
\end{enumerate}
\end{remark}

\section{The Central Result}

\begin{conjecture}[Optimal Agentic Trading as MVUE]
\label{conj:mvue}
A multi-domain Kalman filter bank operating over economic,
financial, fiscal, portfolio, fundamental, market, and sector
observation channels with regime-adaptive process noise, gated by
the seven-dimensional state charge vector and driven by
confidence-weighted agent signals, converges to the
minimum-variance unbiased estimator of the latent market state
under finite capital constraints.  Formally:

\begin{equation}
  \boxed{
    \text{Optimal Agentic Trading}
    \;=\;
    \text{MVUE of } \mathbf{X}_t
    \;\text{ given partial, noisy, multi-domain observations}
  }
  \label{eq:mvue-conjecture}
\end{equation}

and the finite capital constraint enforces the MVUE regime by
preventing over-confident deployment, exactly as a poorly tuned
Kalman gain destabilises state estimation.
\end{conjecture}

\section{The Priority Ordering}

\begin{theorem}[Priority Ordering]
\label{thm:priority}
Under the seven-state gating architecture and the Kalman filter
isomorphism, the following strict priority ordering holds:
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\text{State Integrity}}_{\text{Ch.~\ref{ch:seven-states}}}
  \;\succ\;
  \underbrace{\text{Filter Stability}}_{\text{Ch.~\ref{ch:covariance}}}
  \;\succ\;
  \underbrace{\text{Signal Quality}}_{\text{Ch.~\ref{ch:kalman-gain}}}
  \;\succ\;
  \underbrace{\text{Allocation Intelligence}}_{\text{Ch.~\ref{ch:gating}}}
  \;\succ\;
  \underbrace{\text{Compounding}}_{\text{Ch.~\ref{ch:objective}}}
  \label{eq:priority-ordering}
\end{equation}
which maps exactly onto:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Survival} \;\succ\; \text{Preservation}
  \;\succ\; \text{Intelligent Allocation}
  \;\succ\; \text{Controlled Risk}
  \;\succ\; \text{Compounding}.
  \label{eq:investment-priority}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The priority ordering is enforced architecturally through the
product gating function $G(\mathbf{X}_t)$: a zero value of any
single state factor overrides all signal quality and allocation
intelligence by setting $G = 0$ and hence $\pi^* = 0$.  This
is a hard architectural constraint, not a soft preference.
The filter stability condition $\rho(\mathbf{F} - \mathbf{K}\mathbf{H}\mathbf{F}) < 1$
is the second-priority constraint: it ensures the filter
converges (survival of the estimation process) before any
signal is trusted.  Signal quality (Kalman gain calibration)
takes priority over allocation optimisation because a
miscalibrated gain can destabilise the filter.  Allocation
intelligence (the constrained optimisation) takes priority
over compounding because sub-optimal but stable allocations
dominate optimal-but-risky allocations in the long run by
the Kelly theorem.  \qed
\end{proof}

\section{The Combined Deployment Conjecture}

\begin{conjecture}[Combined Conjecture: Kalman Filter + State Gating]
\label{conj:combined}
Let $\mathbf{X}_t \in [0,1]^7$ be the composite state vector,
$S_t \in [-1,1]$ the ensemble aggregate signal, and
$K_t^{(\mathrm{Kalman})}$ the investment Kalman gain.  The
optimal capital deployment policy is:
\begin{equation}
  \pi^*(\mathbf{X}_t, S_t) \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    K_t^{(\mathrm{Kalman})} \cdot C_{\mathrm{available}}
    \cdot \prod_{d=1}^{7} g_d(\mathcal{S}_t^{(d)}) &
    \text{if } \mathcal{S}_t^{(d)} \geq \mathcal{S}_{\min}^{(d)}
    \;\forall\, d, \\
    0 & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
  \label{eq:combined-policy}
\end{equation}
The combined conjecture states that this policy achieves
superior risk-adjusted compounding under finite capital
constraints relative to single-domain or unconstrained systems,
for the following reasons:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{When all states are charged and the signal is strong}:
        maximum deployment — favourable state plus strong evidence.
  \item \textbf{When all states are charged but the signal is weak}:
        moderate deployment — favourable state but low innovation.
  \item \textbf{When states are mixed but the signal is strong}:
        reduced deployment — signal is strong but the system is
        partially degraded.
  \item \textbf{When any state is critically discharged}:
        zero deployment — circuit-breaker override regardless
        of signal.
\end{enumerate}
\end{conjecture}

\begin{remark}[Conclusion of the Conjecture]
\label{rem:conjecture-conclusion}
The expected conclusion is not that the filter eliminates market
uncertainty.  It is that the Kalman discipline —
\emph{prediction before observation, evidence-weighted updating,
covariance-based deployment scaling, and recursive reputation
adaptation} — converts the finite capital constraint from a
limitation into a precision instrument: a system that computes,
at every step, exactly how much of its scarce capital should be
placed at risk given the information currently available, and
the seven-dimensional state charge vector that specifies how
healthy the investment environment currently is.

The finite capital constraint, enforced by the seven-state
gating function, is the investment-domain analogue of the
battery management system's State-of-Charge protection
circuit.  It transforms the multi-agent Kalman filter from a
pure signal-processing machine into a survival-first,
compounding-second capital engine.
\end{remark}
